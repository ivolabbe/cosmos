## Definition
Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957, was the world's first artificial Earth satellite — a polished metal sphere roughly the size of a beach ball that orbited Earth for three months, transmitting radio beeps detectable worldwide, and inaugurating the Space Age and the US–USSR space race. The word "sputnik" derives from Russian, combining the prefix for "fellow" with "putnik" (traveller), meaning "fellow traveller" — a direct parallel of the Latin root "satelles" underlying the English word "satellite."

## Full Content

- Sputnik 1 was a hollow sphere 58 cm in diameter, constructed from an aluminium-magnesium-titanium alloy with a highly polished surface designed to reflect sunlight and assist visual tracking from Earth [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- It carried two radio transmitters broadcasting on 20.005 MHz and 40.002 MHz, producing the iconic "beep-beep" pulses heard worldwide by amateur radio operators and broadcast on commercial radio [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The timing and duration of the radio pulses encoded temperature and pressure data from inside the satellite: changes in pulse pattern indicated changes in the internal environment; signal cessation would indicate thermal failure or puncture by a micrometeorite [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- Three silver-zinc batteries powered the transmitters; designed for a 2-week life, they lasted 22 days; the inert satellite then remained in orbit for a further ~2.5 months before re-entry [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- Two pairs of whip antennas (2.4 m and 2.9 m long) trailed behind the sphere; the team included antenna designer Mikhail Krayushkin, battery developer Nikolai Lidorenko, and transmitter engineer Vyacheslav Lappo [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The satellite was designed as a simplified "elementary satellite" (Russian: PS-1, Prosteyshiy Sputnik) after the more ambitious "Object D" scientific payload proved too complex to be ready in time [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The launch rocket was the 8K71PS, a stripped-down variant of the R-7 Semyorka — the world's first intercontinental ballistic missile — with the nuclear warhead replaced by the satellite [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- Sputnik 1 was visible to the naked eye as it passed overhead (observers were actually seeing the much larger rocket booster, not the small satellite itself) [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- Tracking was accomplished by Soviet ground stations, the Jodrell Bank (Lovell) radio telescope in Britain, and amateur radio operators worldwide [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The 65.1° orbital inclination meant Sputnik 1 passed over most of Earth's populated land surface during its 3-month mission [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The launch — dubbed the "Sputnik crisis" in the US — shattered American confidence in technological superiority; the implied capability to deliver nuclear warheads via ICBM across intercontinental distances was the core fear [history.state.gov/milestones]
- Within months, the US Congress passed the National Defense Education Act (1958) boosting science and mathematics funding; the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA, later DARPA) was established in February 1958 [history.state.gov/milestones; NASA history]
- NASA was created on 1 October 1958, formed from the National Advisory Committee for Aeronautics (NACA); the National Aeronautics and Space Act was signed by President Eisenhower on 29 July 1958 [Wikipedia/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Act]
- The US Explorer 1 satellite launched 31 January 1958 and discovered the Van Allen radiation belts — America's first significant scientific result from the space race [NASA history]
- Sputnik 2 (3 November 1957, ~500 kg) carried Laika, a stray Moscow dog and the first living creature in orbit; she died from cabin overheating within 5–7 hours; the capsule reentered 14 April 1958 [Wikipedia/Sputnik_2]
- Sputnik 3 (15 May 1958, 1,327 kg) was a comprehensive scientific laboratory with 12 instruments (mass spectrometers, Geiger counters, fluxgate magnetometers); it weighed roughly 100 times more than the heaviest US satellite then flying [Wikipedia/Sputnik_3]
- Only Sputnik 1, 2, and 3 were officially named by the Soviets; spacecraft designated "Sputnik 4–25" in the West bore different official Soviet names (Korabl-Sputnik, Venera, Mars, etc.) whose names were unknown to Western observers at the time [Wikipedia/Sputnik program]

## Historical Context

**International Geophysical Year (IGY) context**
- The IGY ran from 1 July 1957 to 31 December 1958 with 67 participating nations; its goal was coordinated global Earth science research [Wikipedia/IGY]
- On 29 July 1955, the US announced Project Vanguard: plans to launch small Earth satellites via the Naval Research Laboratory during the IGY [Wikipedia/IGY]
- Four days later, Soviet scientist Leonid Sedov announced at a Copenhagen conference that the USSR intended to launch a satellite in the "near future" [Wikipedia/IGY]
- Both superpowers thus publicly committed to satellite launches before 4 October 1957; the competitive context was set well in advance [Wikipedia/IGY]

**Sergei Korolev — the secret architect**
- Korolev served as "Chief Designer" (Russ: glavny konstruktor) of the Soviet missile and space program; his identity was deliberately kept secret from the West throughout the Cold War to prevent assassination attempts [Wikipedia/Korolev]
- His name was publicly revealed only after his death on 14 January 1966, aged 59 [Wikipedia/Korolev]
- Arrested in 1938 during Stalin's Great Purge on false charges, Korolev spent months in a Kolyma gold mine before being moved to a prison research facility and released in 1944; the physical damage was lasting [Wikipedia/Korolev]
- Despite this, Korolev rose to lead Soviet rocket development, creating the R-7 ICBM; he proposed using it for an orbital satellite on 17 December 1954 [Wikipedia/Korolev; Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- The Council of Ministers approved the simplified PS-1 design on 15 February 1957, replacing the more ambitious Object D [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]

**The launch and immediate aftermath**
- Sputnik 1 lifted off from Baikonur's Site 1/5 at 19:28:34 UTC on 4 October 1957; Soviet state radio announced the achievement while radio operators and the public worldwide listened to its signal [Wikipedia/Sputnik_1]
- US President Eisenhower publicly downplayed the launch but authorised rapid acceleration of military space investment behind the scenes [history.state.gov/milestones]
- The US Vanguard rocket failed publicly in December 1957, compounding American embarrassment and public anxiety [history.state.gov/milestones]
- Explorer 1 (31 January 1958) and NASA's creation (1 October 1958) were direct institutional responses [Wikipedia/NASA Act; NASA history]
- Fears of a "missile gap" between US and Soviet capabilities became a major political issue, influencing the 1960 presidential election [history.state.gov/milestones]

**Naming and language legacy**
- "Sputnik" entered Western languages as a generic term for any Soviet satellite and briefly as slang for anything orbiting or new [Britannica]
- The launch is widely regarded as one of the defining moments of the Cold War, reshaping education policy, defence spending, and international scientific cooperation for decades [history.state.gov/milestones]
