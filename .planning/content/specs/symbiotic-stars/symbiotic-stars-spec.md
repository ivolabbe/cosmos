## Definition
A symbiotic star is an interacting binary system in which a cool red giant and a hot compact companion — almost always a white dwarf — are close enough that the giant's stellar wind or overflowing envelope partly ionises the surrounding gas, producing a spectrum that simultaneously displays absorption features of a cool star and high-ionisation emission lines normally seen only in very hot objects.

## Full Content

- Symbiotic stars are interacting binary systems pairing a cool, evolved giant (usually M-type, occasionally K-type or carbon star) with a hot, compact object — in ~99% of cases a white dwarf, rarely a neutron star [arXiv:2504.16825; Wikipedia]
- The giant loses mass via a slow stellar wind (most common) or, in closer systems, via Roche lobe overflow; wind Roche lobe overflow (WRLO) is an intermediate mode in which wind material fills the Roche lobe and transfers through the inner Lagrangian point, giving accretion rates ~100× that of simple Bondi–Hoyle accretion [arXiv:2508.06727; arXiv:2501.07067]
- The hot white dwarf companion, with surface temperatures typically reaching tens of thousands to ~200,000 K, photo-ionises the surrounding wind nebula, generating strong emission lines of H, He, O, Ne, and higher-ionisation species [Wikipedia; arXiv:2504.16825]
- Spectral diagnostics: presence of [O III] (67% of confirmed systems), He II (80%), [Fe VI] (43%), and Raman-scattered O VI (~40%) all indicate the co-existence of a hot ionising source within a cool giant wind [arXiv:2504.16825]
- About one-quarter of known Galactic symbiotics have been seen in at least one optical outburst, during which the system can brighten by several magnitudes in a matter of weeks [arXiv:2504.16825; Wikipedia]
- Outbursts are driven by instabilities in the accretion disk or episodes of enhanced mass transfer; they can produce nova-like shell ejections (thermonuclear runaways on the white dwarf surface) in recurrent novae such as RS Ophiuchi and T Coronae Borealis [Bollimpalli et al. 2018; A&A 2025]
- Jets: collimated bipolar outflows — the best-studied being in R Aquarii — are launched preferentially during active phases; jets in R Aquarii extend ≥248 billion miles and travel at >450 km/s [star-facts.com; ESA/Hubble heic2413]
- R Aquarii (distance ~711 light-years) is the nearest known symbiotic star and contains a Mira-type red giant (>400 R☉, pulsation period ~390 days) orbiting a white dwarf with a ~44-year period; Hubble observations from 2014–2023 show the nebular filaments evolving in real time [star-facts.com; ESA/Hubble 2024]
- Z Andromedae (prototype of the class) varies by up to 4 magnitudes at irregular intervals; major outbursts recur every 10–20 years and show a temporary B-type shell spectrum during maximum [daviddarling.info; AAVSO]
- Symbiotic stars have long been proposed as progenitors of Type Ia supernovae via the single-degenerate channel: a white dwarf accretes enough mass to approach the Chandrasekhar limit (~1.4 M☉); the recurrent nova RS Ophiuchi hosts a white dwarf already near this mass, and supernova PTF11kx appears to have originated from a symbiotic nova progenitor [arxiv.org/abs/2511.19948; Science 2012]
- However, a 2025 study finds zero evidence of red giant companions in 189 normal Type Ia supernovae remnants, casting doubt on whether symbiotic channels contribute significantly to the normal SN Ia rate [arXiv:2511.19948]
- Symbiotic stars with neutron stars (e.g. V934 Her / GX 1+4) form a distinct sub-class; the neutron star accretes from the giant wind and emits X-ray pulsations [Wikipedia; arXiv:2504.16825]
- Modern surveys (IPHAS, SHS, VPHAS+, Gaia, TESS) are rapidly expanding the known population; TESS flickering detections have confirmed 13 new accreting-only systems, and ~80% of accreting-only symbiotics now show flickering signatures consistent with an accretion disk [arXiv:2504.16825]

## Historical Context

- Annie Cannon (Harvard) recognised the spectral peculiarity of objects now classified as symbiotic stars around the turn of the 20th century through objective-prism surveys [Wikipedia]
- Early spectroscopic work by Paul W. Merrill (1919, 1932) and J.S. Plaskett (1931) established that the high-ionisation lines on cool giant spectra require an unseen hot source; Merrill coined the term "symbiotic stars" at an American Astronomical Society meeting at Yerkes Observatory in 1941 [arXiv:2504.16825]
- Z Andromedae was discovered in 1901 by Williamina Fleming on Harvard College Observatory photographic plates; it remains the only officially-recognised sub-class in the General Catalogue of Variable Stars [daviddarling.info]
- The first systematic catalogues were compiled by Sanduleak & Stephenson (1973) and Allen (1978) using objective-prism surveys; Belczynski et al. (2000) produced the first comprehensive digital catalogue (188 confirmed + 30 suspected systems) incorporated into HEASARC [HEASARC; arXiv:2504.16825]
- R Aquarii was first observed by Hubble in 1990; its jets and surrounding Mira nebula have been monitored continuously, providing the best time-resolved record of jet evolution in any symbiotic system [star-facts.com; ESA/Hubble 2024]

## Sub-types / Classifications

### Infrared continuum types (Kenyon 1986)

| Type | Characteristic | Cool Component |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| S-type (stellar) | Stellar near-IR continuum; giant directly visible; ~80% of Galactic systems | Normal M, K, S, or carbon giant; semi-regular or irregular variable |
| D-type (dusty) | Optically thick warm dust shell (700–1000 K) peaks at ~2–2.5 μm; giant not directly visible; ~15% | Mira-type long-period variable |
| D'-type | Cold dust, far-IR colors resembling compact planetary nebulae; ~3% | Extreme OH/IR or Mira variable |

- "Yellow symbiotics" are S-type systems with K-giant donors (e.g. LT Del, AG Dra), cooler and more luminous than typical M-giant systems [arXiv:2504.16825]
- Carbon-star symbiotic systems: 10 confirmed in the Milky Way; more common proportionally in the Magellanic Clouds [arXiv:2504.16825]

### X-ray emission types (Muerset et al. 1997)

| X-ray type | Emission | Origin |
|------------|----------|--------|
| α-type | Supersoft (< 0.4 keV) | Quasi-steady nuclear burning on white dwarf |
| β-type | Soft thermal (< 2.4 keV) | Colliding stellar winds; ~33% of X-ray symbiotics |
| δ-type | Hard thermal (> 2.4 keV) | Boundary layer between accretion disk and white dwarf; ~20% |
| β/δ combined | Both regimes | ~20% |

### By compact object

- **White dwarf symbiotic** (vast majority): accretion-powered, can undergo nova outbursts
- **Neutron star symbiotic** (e.g. GX 1+4, V934 Her): X-ray pulsars accreting from giant wind
- **Symbiotic recurrent novae** (e.g. RS Oph, T CrB): white dwarf mass near Chandrasekhar limit, outbursts on decade timescales
