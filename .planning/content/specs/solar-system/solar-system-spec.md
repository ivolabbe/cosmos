# Solar System — Article Spec

## Category
solar-system

## Definition
The Solar System is the gravitationally bound system centered on the Sun, comprising eight planets, five officially recognized dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, millions of asteroids, several billion comets, and the interplanetary medium. It formed approximately 4.6 billion years ago from the collapse of a giant molecular cloud.

## Full Content

### Formation & Age
The Solar System formed approximately **4.6 billion years ago** through gravitational collapse of a portion of a giant molecular cloud. The prevailing explanation is the **nebular hypothesis**, in which a collapsing molecular cloud flattened into a protoplanetary disk. Most material concentrated at the center to form the Sun, while remaining material eventually became planets, moons, asteroids, and other bodies through accretion—the collision and merging of dust grains into increasingly larger bodies.

**Source:** Wikipedia: Formation and evolution of the Solar System; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_and_evolution_of_the_Solar_System

### Composition & Mass
The **Sun dominates the Solar System**, containing **99.86% of the total mass** of the system. The Sun sustains nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen into helium and radiating enormous energy into space. Jupiter and Saturn together contain nearly 90% of the non-stellar mass.

**Source:** Britannica: Solar System; https://www.britannica.com/science/solar-system

### The Eight Planets

The eight planets divide into two distinct groups based on composition and location:

#### Terrestrial (Inner) Planets
The four rocky terrestrial planets orbit closest to the Sun: **Mercury** (smallest and nearest to the Sun), **Venus**, **Earth**, and **Mars**. These planets have solid surfaces, high density, and few or no natural satellites. Venus is the hottest planet with an average surface temperature of **464°C (867°F)**.

**Sources:** 
- NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/
- Britannica: Solar System; https://www.britannica.com/science/solar-system

#### Giant Planets
The four outer planets lack hard surfaces but feature swirling atmospheres above a core:

**Gas Giants:** Jupiter (the largest planet in the Solar System) and Saturn feature thick atmospheres of hydrogen and helium with ring systems.

**Ice Giants:** Uranus and Neptune are composed primarily of ices surrounding a rocky core.

**Source:** NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/

### Dwarf Planets
Five officially recognized dwarf planets exist: **Ceres** (located in the asteroid belt), **Pluto**, **Eris**, **Haumea**, and **Makemake**. Pluto was reclassified from planetary status to dwarf planet in 2006 after the International Astronomical Union established that planets must "clear the neighbourhood around their orbit"—a criterion Pluto cannot meet.

**Sources:**
- NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/
- Britannica: Solar System; https://www.britannica.com/science/solar-system

### Asteroid Belt
The **asteroid belt** is a torus-shaped region between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter, spanning approximately **2.06 to 3.3–4.2 AU** from the Sun. The belt contains three primary asteroid types: C-type carbonaceous asteroids (comprising over 75% of visible asteroids, found in outer regions), S-type silicate asteroids (more common in the inner belt within 2.5 AU), and M-type metal-rich asteroids (found in the middle belt). The belt's total mass equals approximately **3% of the Moon's mass**, with the four largest objects (Ceres, Vesta, Pallas, and Hygiea) comprising about 62% of this total.

**Source:** Wikipedia: Asteroid belt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asteroid_belt

### Kuiper Belt
The **Kuiper belt** is a circumstellar disc in the outer Solar System extending from **Neptune's orbit at 30 AU to approximately 50 AU** from the Sun. Objects in this region consist largely of frozen materials called "ices," including **methane, ammonia, and water**, in the extreme cold around **50 Kelvin**. The belt contains thousands of known objects, with estimates suggesting over 100,000 KBOs exceeding 100 km in diameter exist. The first Kuiper belt object beyond Pluto was discovered in 1992. Despite its vast extent, the belt's total mass is estimated at less than 2% of Earth's mass.

**Source:** Wikipedia: Kuiper belt; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuiper_belt

### Oort Cloud
The **Oort cloud** is theorized to be a spherical shell of billions of icy planetesimals surrounding the Sun at distances ranging from **2,000 to 200,000 AU**. The cloud comprises two regions: the Inner Oort Cloud (Hills Cloud)—a disc-shaped region extending 2,000–20,000 AU from the Sun—and the Outer Oort Cloud—a spherical region spanning 20,000–50,000 AU, with some estimates extending to 100,000–200,000 AU. Objects consist primarily of ices such as **water, methane, ethane, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen cyanide**, with roughly 1–2% asteroids mixed with these icy bodies. The outer edge is defined by the **tidal truncation radius**, where the Sun's gravity yields to galactic forces, representing the cosmographic boundary of the Solar System between **100,000 and 200,000 AU**.

**Source:** Wikipedia: Oort cloud; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oort_cloud

### Additional Objects
The Solar System contains approximately **1.4 million catalogued asteroids** and about **4,000 comets**, along with hundreds of moons orbiting various planets and even some asteroids. The **interplanetary medium** consists of tenuous plasma and dust extending outward from the Sun.

**Source:** NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/

### Orbital Characteristics
All planets orbit the Sun in **elliptical orbits in the same direction that the Sun rotates**. The Solar System orbits the galactic center at roughly **515,000 mph (828,000 km/h)**, requiring approximately **230 million years** to complete one full orbit around the Milky Way.

**Source:** NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/

### Future Evolution
In roughly **5 billion years**, the Sun will exhaust its hydrogen fuel and expand into a red giant phase. Eventually it will shed its outer layers and become a white dwarf. Over extraordinarily long timescales, passing stars will gradually strip away the Sun's remaining planets, ultimately ending the Solar System as a meaningful entity.

**Source:** Wikipedia: Formation and evolution of the Solar System; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formation_and_evolution_of_the_Solar_System

### Cosmic Context
The nearest star, **Proxima Centauri**, lies **4.25 light-years** away from the Solar System. Our Solar System resides within the Milky Way galaxy, specifically in "a small, partial arm called the Orion Arm, or Orion Spur."

**Source:** NASA Solar System Exploration; https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/

## Historical Context

### Pre-Scientific Models
Ancient astronomers placed Earth at the center of the cosmos, with celestial bodies orbiting around it. This geocentric model dominated Western astronomy until challenged by heliocentric theory in the 16th–17th centuries.

### Heliocentric Revolution
**Nicolaus Copernicus** proposed the heliocentric model in 1543, placing the Sun at the center. **Galileo Galilei's** telescopic observations in the early 1600s provided evidence supporting heliocentrism. **Isaac Newton's** laws of motion and universal gravitation (1687) provided the mathematical foundation explaining planetary orbits.

### Modern Era
The discovery of **Uranus (1781)** by William Herschel expanded known planetary boundaries. **Neptune** was discovered in 1846 through mathematical predictions. The 20th century brought discovery of the **asteroid belt's structure**, the **Kuiper belt** (confirmed 1992 with discovery of the first KBO beyond Pluto), and theoretical understanding of the **Oort cloud** (proposed by Jan Oort in 1950). In **2006**, the International Astronomical Union officially defined "planet," reclassifying Pluto as a dwarf planet.
