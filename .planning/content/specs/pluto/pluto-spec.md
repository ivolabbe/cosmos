## Definition
Pluto is a dwarf planet in the Kuiper Belt — a ring of icy bodies beyond the orbit of Neptune — composed primarily of rock and ice, and roughly two-thirds the size of Earth's Moon. Discovered in 1930 and long considered the ninth planet, it was reclassified by the International Astronomical Union in 2006 because it has not gravitationally cleared its orbital neighbourhood.

## Full Content

### Physical Description
- Pluto is the ninth-largest and tenth-most-massive object directly orbiting the Sun [Wikipedia]
- Composition: roughly 2/3 rock and 1/3 ice by mass; density (1.853 g/cm³) is intermediate between ice and rock [Wikipedia]
- Interior likely differentiated: rocky silicate core ~1,700 km in diameter, surrounded by a water-ice mantle [Wikipedia]
- New Horizons data provide compelling evidence for a subsurface liquid water ocean 100–180 km thick at the core–mantle boundary, sustained by radioactive decay heating [Wikipedia / Earthsky]
- 2024 research indicates this putative ocean may be saltier and denser than Earth's seawater [Earthsky 2024]
- Surface albedo is high and strikingly varied — surface contrast rivals Saturn's moon Iapetus [Wikipedia]

### Surface Features
- New Horizons (2015) revealed unexpectedly diverse terrain: plains, mountains, valleys, craters, and possible cryovolcanoes [NASA Science]
- **Sputnik Planitia** (western lobe of heart-shaped Tombaugh Regio): ~1,400 × 1,200 km basin of nitrogen ice; no detectable craters, implying surface age <10 million years [Wikipedia: Sputnik Planitia]
- Polygonal convection cells in Sputnik Planitia average ~33 km across with ~100 m relief; convection rate ~13.8 cm/year — geologically active by solar system standards [Wikipedia: Sputnik Planitia]
- Water-ice mountains rise 2–3.5 km above surrounding plains (comparable to the Rocky Mountains) [NASA Science / Space.com]
- Valleys up to 600 km long; craters up to 260 km in diameter exist in older terrains [NASA Science]
- Plains: >98% nitrogen ice with traces of methane and carbon monoxide; mountains: water ice [Wikipedia]
- **Tombaugh Regio** ("the Heart"): bright, nitrogen-rich region; **Cthulhu Regio** ("the Whale"): dark, tholin-rich equatorial belt [Wikipedia]
- **Tholins**: complex organic compounds from UV irradiation of CH₄/N₂; create reddish colour; possibly 10–30 m thick in places [Wikipedia / Space.com]
- Possible cryovolcanic features: Wright Mons and Piccard Mons are candidate cryovolcanoes [Wikipedia]

### Atmosphere
- Thin atmosphere; surface pressure ~1.0 Pa (~10⁻⁵ of Earth's) [Wikipedia]
- Composition: nitrogen (dominant), methane, carbon monoxide [Wikipedia / NASA Science]
- Comet-like behaviour: atmosphere expands as Pluto approaches perihelion and collapses when it recedes [NASA Science]
- Extends up to ~1,600 km above the surface [Space.com]
- Blue atmospheric haze (visible in New Horizons backlit images) caused by tholins scattering sunlight [Space.com]
- Nitrogen frost condenses on the surface during colder seasons [Wikipedia]

### Orbital Characteristics
- Resides in the Kuiper Belt, a trans-Neptunian disc of icy bodies extending from ~30 to ~50 AU [Wikipedia]
- Between 1979 and 1999, Pluto's orbit brought it closer to the Sun than Neptune; the 3:2 mean-motion resonance with Neptune prevents any collision [Wikipedia / NASA Science]
- 3:2 Neptune resonance: every 2 Pluto orbits, Neptune completes exactly 3; Pluto belongs to the dynamical class "plutinos" [Wikipedia]
- Orbital inclination 17.16° — far higher than any classical planet — and eccentricity 0.2488 [Wikipedia]
- Retrograde rotation (east to west), like Venus and Uranus; axial tilt 120° means Pluto effectively spins on its side [Wikipedia / NASA Science]

### Moon System
- Five known moons; all named for underworld mythology [NASA Science]
- **Charon** (discovered 1978): diameter 1,212 km — the largest moon relative to its parent body in the Solar System [Wikipedia: Charon]
- Pluto–Charon system is a quasi-binary: the orbital barycenter lies outside Pluto; both bodies are mutually tidally locked — each always shows the same face to the other [Wikipedia: Charon]
- Charon composition: ~55% rock, ~45% ice; density 1.705 g/cm³; separated from Pluto by 19,596 km [Wikipedia: Charon]
- Charon's north polar dark region (Neverland Regio) is reddish tholins; equatorial Serenity Chasma extends >1,000 km [Wikipedia: Charon]
- Nix and Hydra: discovered 2005 (Hubble); Kerberos (2011) and Styx (2012) round out the five [Wikipedia]

### New Horizons Mission
- NASA's New Horizons launched 19 January 2006; first close flyby of Pluto on 14 July 2015 at 12,500 km altitude, speed >84,000 km/h [Wikipedia: New Horizons]
- Seven instruments including LORRI (long-range camera), Ralph (colour/infrared mapper), and Alice (UV spectrometer) [Wikipedia: New Horizons]
- Mission transformed Pluto from a point of light to a geologically complex world, overturning expectations of a dead, inert body [NASA Science]
- After Pluto, New Horizons flew by Kuiper Belt Object Arrokoth on 1 January 2019; as of 2025 the spacecraft is ~62 AU from Earth, continuing KBO science [Wikipedia: New Horizons]

### Kuiper Belt and Reclassification Context
- Pluto's mass is only 0.07× the combined mass of all other objects sharing its orbital zone; Earth's is 1.7 million times — the core reason Pluto fails the IAU "clearing the neighbourhood" criterion [Wikipedia]
- Discovery of Eris (2005), a trans-Neptunian object slightly more massive than Pluto, catalysed the 2006 IAU vote to define "dwarf planet" [Wikipedia]
- Many planetary scientists, including New Horizons PI Alan Stern, reject the IAU definition and continue to regard Pluto as a planet; debate remains active [Wikipedia]

## Historical Context

- **1905–1930 — Prediction**: Percival Lowell predicted a "Planet X" beyond Neptune to explain apparent perturbations in Uranus's orbit; the Lowell Observatory search began in 1906 but Lowell died in 1916 without finding it.
- **18 February 1930 — Discovery**: Clyde W. Tombaugh (age 24, a self-taught Kansas farm boy) found Pluto by comparing photographic plates using a blink comparator at Lowell Observatory, Flagstaff, Arizona. The discovery was publicly announced on 13 March 1930 — Lowell's birthday.
- **Naming (March 1930)**: "Pluto" suggested by Venetia Burney, an 11-year-old schoolgirl from Oxford, submitted via her grandfather Falconer Madan to the observatory. The name was unanimously chosen: it honours the Roman god of the underworld (fitting for a dark, distant world) and the first two letters, PL, honour Percival Lowell.
- **1955**: Rotation period determined to be ~6.39 days.
- **22 June 1978**: James W. Christy at the US Naval Observatory Flagstaff Station discovered Charon on photographic plates, revealing Pluto was much smaller than previously estimated (earlier mass estimates had included Charon's contribution).
- **1988**: Pluto's atmosphere first confirmed from stellar occultation data.
- **2005**: Hubble Space Telescope images revealed Nix and Hydra.
- **24 August 2006**: IAU formally reclassified Pluto as a "dwarf planet" — a new category requiring orbit around the Sun and hydrostatic equilibrium, but not clearing the neighbourhood. The discovery of Eris earlier that year prompted the redefinition.
- **2011–2012**: Hubble discovered Kerberos (2011) and Styx (2012), completing the five-moon system.
- **14 July 2015**: New Horizons' closest approach to Pluto, revealing a geologically active, diverse world.
- **1 January 2019**: New Horizons flyby of Arrokoth, extending Kuiper Belt exploration.
- **May 2024**: Research published suggesting Pluto's subsurface ocean is saltier and denser than Earth's seawater.

## Sub-types / Classifications

Pluto belongs to several overlapping categories:

1. **Dwarf planet** (IAU 2006): Orbits the Sun, is in hydrostatic equilibrium (roughly spherical), but has not cleared its orbital neighbourhood. Pluto is the prototype dwarf planet alongside Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres.
2. **Kuiper Belt Object (KBO) / Trans-Neptunian Object (TNO)**: An icy body orbiting beyond Neptune in the Kuiper Belt (~30–50 AU). Pluto is the largest KBO by diameter.
3. **Plutino**: A KBO locked in the 3:2 orbital resonance with Neptune (2 Pluto orbits per 3 Neptune orbits). Pluto is the prototype of this dynamical class (~200 known members).
4. **Ice dwarf**: Informal category for outer Solar System dwarf planets dominated by ice and rock, distinguishing them from rocky/metallic dwarf planets like Ceres.
5. **Binary dwarf planet system**: Pluto and Charon orbit their common barycenter (which lies outside Pluto's surface), making the pair a de facto binary system — unique among dwarf planets.
