## Definition

Nearby stars are the stellar objects — including main-sequence stars, white dwarfs, brown dwarfs, and sub-brown dwarfs — located within roughly 20 light-years (about 6 parsecs) of the Sun, forming what astronomers call the Sun's immediate stellar neighbourhood. The closest individual star to the Sun is Proxima Centauri at 4.2465 light-years, part of the Alpha Centauri triple system.

## Full Content

### What "Nearby Stars" Means
- "Nearby" has no rigid definition, but conventionally refers to stars within roughly 10–20 light-years (3–6 parsecs) of the Sun [Wikipedia]
- The most used survey boundary is 20 ly (6.13 pc); within this, 131 objects are known in 94 systems [Wikipedia]
- Distances are measured by stellar parallax — the tiny angular shift of a star against background stars as Earth orbits the Sun [Wikipedia]
- Proxima Centauri's parallax is ~0.762 arcsec, equivalent to observing a dime at a distance of ~6 km [HyperPhysics]
- Modern parallax measurements from the Hipparcos and Gaia satellites have dramatically improved precision for all nearby stars [Wikipedia]
- The RECONS consortium (founded 1994 by Todd Henry at Georgia State University) systematically surveys all stars within 10 parsecs (later expanded to 25 parsecs) and publishes the "List of the 100 nearest star systems" [Wikipedia, RECONS]

### The Solar Stellar Neighbourhood
- The Sun resides in a low-density region of the Milky Way; average separation between stellar systems locally is ~4–5 light-years [Wikipedia]
- This sparse environment contrasts sharply with globular clusters: globular cluster cores have mass densities ~500× higher than the solar neighbourhood [Wikipedia, Stellar density]
- The Sun sits within the Local Interstellar Cloud (~30 ly across, temperature ~7,000 K, density ~0.3 atoms/cm³), which itself is inside the Local Bubble, a low-density cavity ≥1,000 ly across [Wikipedia, Local Bubble; Solar neighborhood]
- The Local Bubble was excavated by multiple supernovae in the Scorpius–Centaurus association 10–20 million years ago [Wikipedia, Local Bubble]
- Over the next 15 million years, an estimated 694 stars may pass within 5 parsecs of the Sun [Wikipedia]

### Dominant Type: Red Dwarf Stars
- Of the 103 main-sequence stars within 20 ly, ~80 (≈78%) are red dwarfs (M spectral class) [Wikipedia]
- Red dwarfs are far less luminous than the Sun: Proxima Centauri emits only 0.16% of the Sun's total luminosity [Wikipedia]
- Red dwarfs are long-lived — lifetimes of tens to hundreds of billions of years — far exceeding the current age of the universe [Wikipedia]
- Most red dwarfs are flare stars: Proxima Centauri and Wolf 359 both exhibit sudden dramatic brightness increases driven by strong magnetic activity [Wikipedia]
- Wolf 359 (M6V, 7.86 ly) emits only ~0.1% of the Sun's power; its surface temperature is ~2,749 K and its magnetic field is hundreds of times stronger than the Sun's [Wikipedia]
- The two nearest brown dwarfs, Luhman 16 A (L8) and B (T1), orbit each other at just 6.50 ly from the Sun [Wikipedia]

### The Alpha Centauri System
- The nearest stellar system to Earth is Alpha Centauri, a triple system comprising Alpha Cen A (G2V, Sun-like), Alpha Cen B (K1V, orange dwarf), and Proxima Centauri (M5.5Ve, red dwarf) [Wikipedia]
- Alpha Cen A and B orbit each other with a period of 79 years; their separation varies from 11.2 to 35.6 AU due to an orbital eccentricity of 0.52 [Wikipedia]
- Proxima Centauri, ~0.2 ly from AB, appears gravitationally bound to the pair with an orbital period of ~550,000 years [Wikipedia]
- Proxima hosts three known/candidate planets: Proxima b (Earth-mass, habitable-zone, 11.2-day orbit), Proxima d (sub-Earth, 5.1-day orbit), and disputed candidate Proxima c [Wikipedia]
- Alpha Centauri is the fourth-brightest object in the night sky (combined apparent magnitude −0.27) [Wikipedia]

### Barnard's Star
- Second-closest system at 5.96 ly; fourth-closest individual star [Wikipedia]
- An M4.0V red dwarf in Ophiuchus: mass 0.162 M☉, luminosity 0.0034 L☉, temperature ~3,195 K [Wikipedia]
- Has the highest known proper motion of any star: 10.3 arcsec/yr, discovered by E.E. Barnard in 1916 [Wikipedia]
- At 7–12 billion years old, it is one of the oldest stars in the solar neighbourhood — roughly twice the Sun's age [Wikipedia]
- Approaches the Sun at ~110 km/s; will reach closest approach (~3.75 ly) in ~9,800 years [Wikipedia]
- Four sub-Earth-mass exoplanets (b, c, d, e) were confirmed by independent teams in 2024–2025 [Wikipedia]

### Sirius — The Brightest Nearby Star
- At 8.71 ly, Sirius is the fifth-closest system to the Sun and by far the brightest star in the night sky (apparent magnitude −1.46; almost twice the brightness of second-place Canopus) [Wikipedia]
- Sirius A is an A1V main-sequence star with mass 2.063 M☉ and luminosity ~25 L☉ [Wikipedia]
- Sirius B is a white dwarf companion (spectral type DA2), mass ~1.02 M☉ in an Earth-sized volume, orbiting A with a 50.1-year period [Wikipedia]
- Its brilliance results from a combination of relative proximity and high intrinsic luminosity; it is not the nearest star to Earth [Wikipedia]
- Known as the "Dog Star," its heliacal rising was used by Ancient Egyptians to predict the Nile's annual flood [Wikipedia]

### Other Notable Nearby Stars
- **Epsilon Eridani** (10.5 ly, K2V): third-closest individual star visible to the naked eye; only ~1.1 billion years old; hosts one confirmed giant planet (AEgir); a popular SETI target [Wikipedia]
- **Tau Ceti** (11.9 ly, G8V): nearest Sun-like single star; ~80% of the Sun's mass; 8–10 billion years old; exoplanet candidates remain disputed [Wikipedia]
- **61 Cygni** (11.4 ly, K5V + K7V binary): first star beyond the Sun to have its distance reliably measured, by Friedrich Bessel in 1838 [Wikipedia]

### Significance and Study
- Nearby stars are the best-studied stellar objects: proximity allows precise parallax, proper motion, radial velocity, and surface-activity measurements [Wikipedia]
- They serve as benchmarks for stellar models — calibrating mass–luminosity, age–activity, and mass–radius relations [Wikipedia]
- Their planetary systems are primary targets for exoplanet searches and potential interstellar probe concepts (e.g., Breakthrough Starshot targets Proxima Centauri) [Wikipedia]
- Red dwarf habitability questions — driven by intense flare activity — are central to the search for life around nearby stars [Wikipedia]

## Historical Context

- **1838**: Friedrich Bessel measures the distance to 61 Cygni by stellar parallax — the first reliable stellar distance measurement beyond the Sun; parallax = 0.314 arcsec [Wikipedia]
- **1839**: Thomas Henderson separately measures Alpha Centauri's parallax from the Cape of Good Hope; his result confirmed Alpha Centauri as the nearest naked-eye star system [Wikipedia]
- **1916**: Edward Emerson Barnard discovers the record proper motion of Barnard's Star while cataloguing high-motion stars using photographic plates [Wikipedia]
- **1930s–1960s**: Systematic proper-motion surveys by Wilhelm Luyten and others catalogue dozens of previously unknown nearby red dwarfs; Peter van de Kamp's claimed planet detections for Barnard's Star (later refuted) drive interest in nearby-star planetary systems [Wikipedia]
- **1989–1993**: ESA's Hipparcos satellite measures parallaxes to milliarcsecond precision for 118,000 stars, refining distances to all nearby stars and completing the census to ~10 pc [Wikipedia]
- **1994**: RECONS (Research Consortium on Nearby Stars) founded at Georgia State University by Todd Henry; begins systematic survey of all stars within 10 pc [Wikipedia, RECONS]
- **2013–present**: ESA's Gaia mission provides microarcsecond parallaxes for over 1 billion stars; Gaia EDR3 and DR3 substantially refine the nearby-star census and discover new nearby objects [Wikipedia]
- **2016**: Proxima b announced — the first confirmed Earth-mass planet in the habitable zone of the nearest star [Wikipedia]
- **2024–2025**: Four sub-Earth-mass exoplanets confirmed around Barnard's Star, making it the closest confirmed multi-planet system [Wikipedia]

## Sub-types / Classifications

Within the population of nearby stars, the main categories are:

| Type | Count within 20 ly | Notes |
|------|---------------------|-------|
| M-dwarf (red dwarf) | ~80 | Most common; faint, cool, long-lived, often flare stars |
| K-dwarf (orange dwarf) | ~8 | Cooler than Sun; include 61 Cygni A/B, Epsilon Eridani |
| G-dwarf (yellow dwarf) | ~2–3 | Sun-like; include Alpha Cen A, Tau Ceti |
| A-dwarf | ~1 | Rare; Sirius A — hot, massive, relatively short-lived |
| White dwarf | 6 | Stellar remnants; include Sirius B, Van Maanen's Star |
| Brown dwarf | 21 | Sub-stellar; include Luhman 16 A/B (6.5 ly — nearest known) |
| Sub-brown dwarf | 1 | Below hydrogen-burning and deuterium-burning limits |

Key groupings:
- **Flare stars**: Red and orange dwarfs that exhibit sudden large luminosity increases from magnetic reconnection events; Proxima Centauri, Wolf 359, UV Ceti are examples within 20 ly
- **Binary/multiple systems**: Many nearby stars are in gravitational pairs or triples; Alpha Centauri (triple), 61 Cygni (binary), Sirius (binary) are examples
- **Single stars**: Barnard's Star, Wolf 359, Tau Ceti — no confirmed stellar-mass companions
