## Definition
Tycho's Nova (SN 1572) was a Type Ia supernova that appeared in the constellation Cassiopeia in November 1572, reaching an apparent magnitude of approximately −4 (rivalling Venus) and remaining visible to the naked eye for about 16 months. Observed most famously by Danish astronomer Tycho Brahe, it demonstrated that the "fixed stars" could change — challenging Aristotelian cosmology and catalysing the revolution in observational astronomy.

## Full Content

- SN 1572 was a thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernova caused by the explosion of a carbon–oxygen white dwarf that accreted material from a companion star until it exceeded the Chandrasekhar limit (~1.4 solar masses) and detonated [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- At peak brightness around 16 November 1572, it reached apparent magnitude −4, comparable to Venus, and was visible in daylight [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- The supernova faded gradually, dropping below naked-eye visibility by March 1574, roughly 16 months after first appearing [Britannica]
- No stellar remnant (neutron star or black hole) remains — Type Ia explosions completely destroy the white dwarf [Britannica]
- The expanding remnant was first detected at radio wavelengths in 1952 by Hanbury Brown and Hazard at 158.5 MHz using the Jodrell Bank Observatory [Wikipedia]
- Optical filaments from the remnant were discovered in the 1960s using the Palomar Mountain telescope [Wikipedia]
- X-ray emission was detected by the Uhuru satellite (designation 4U 0022+63) [Wikipedia]
- Chandra X-ray Observatory observations (207 hours in 2009) revealed X-ray "stripes" — a pattern never before seen in a supernova remnant — suggesting particle acceleration to energies ~100 times greater than the Large Hadron Collider achieves [Chandra 2011]
- Low-energy X-rays trace the expanding supernova debris; high-energy X-rays outline the blast wave and its shell of ultra-energetic electrons [Chandra 2011; APOD]
- The forward shock expands at 4,000–5,000 km/s, decelerating where it encounters denser interstellar clouds [Wikipedia]
- In 2008, Krause et al. obtained a spectrum of the original supernova light from a scattered light echo — photons from the 1572 explosion reflected off interstellar dust and arriving at Earth centuries later — confirming SN 1572 as a "normal" Type Ia supernova [Krause et al. 2008, Nature 456, 617]
- The light-echo spectrum showed a strong Ca II infrared triplet feature at velocities exceeding 20,000 km/s, suggesting asphericity in the explosion [Krause et al. 2008]
- In 2004, Ruiz-Lapuente et al. identified Tycho G, a G2-type (Sun-like) star, as the probable surviving companion of the white dwarf progenitor; Hubble measured its motion against the background sky [ESA/Hubble heic0415; Wikipedia]
- Tycho G's peculiar velocity was initially estimated at 136 km/s but revised downward to ~56 km/s using Gaia DR2 data; its distance of ~6,400 ly from Gaia is somewhat less than some remnant distance estimates, leaving the association debated [Wikipedia]
- The remnant spans ~8 arcminutes (~55 light-years in diameter) and is a bright X-ray and radio source [Chandra; Wikipedia]
- Spitzer Space Telescope infrared observations revealed newly synthesised dust in the ejected material as well as heated pre-existing interstellar dust [Chandra 2009]

## Historical Context

- Multiple European observers recorded the star's appearance in early November 1572; Tycho Brahe first observed it on 11 November 1572 from Herrevad Abbey in Denmark [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- Other documented observers include Jerónimo Muñoz (Spain), Christopher Clavius, Thomas Digges, John Dee, Francesco Maurolico, and Tadeáš Hájek [Wikipedia]
- Some European observers claimed sightings as early as August 1572 [Britannica]
- Tycho published his observations in *De Nova et Nullius Aevi Memoria Prius Visa Stella* ("Concerning the Star, New and Never Before Seen in the Life or Memory of Anyone") in 1573, with reprints overseen by Kepler in 1602 and 1610 [Wikipedia]
- Tycho's careful measurements showed no detectable diurnal parallax, proving the object lay far beyond the Moon — at the distance of the fixed stars [Wikipedia; APS]
- This finding directly contradicted Aristotelian cosmology, which held the celestial sphere to be perfect and unchanging [Wikipedia; Britannica]
- *De Nova Stella* is regarded as one of the most important books in the history of astronomy; it also gave us the word "nova" (later extended to "supernova") [Wikipedia]
- The event spurred demand for better astrometric star catalogues and more precise observing instruments, catalysing the astronomical revolution that followed [Wikipedia]

## Sub-types / Classifications

- SN 1572 is classified as a Type Ia supernova — the thermonuclear detonation of a white dwarf in a binary system [Krause et al. 2008]
- Within the Type Ia class, light-echo spectroscopy confirmed it as a "normal" (Branch-normal) Type Ia, the majority subclass used as standardisable candles for cosmological distance measurements [Krause et al. 2008]
- The remnant is classified as a shell-type supernova remnant (no central compact object) [Wikipedia]
