## Definition
Andromeda is a large constellation of the northern sky, sitting at approximately 1 hour right ascension and 40° north declination, best known as the home of the Andromeda Galaxy (M31) — the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way and the most distant object visible to the naked eye. It represents the mythological princess chained to a rock as a sea-monster's sacrifice, rescued by Perseus.

## Full Content

### Constellation basics
- Andromeda occupies 722 square degrees — the 19th largest of the 88 IAU-recognised constellations, about 1.75% of the sky [Wikipedia]
- IAU three-letter abbreviation: And; adopted in 1922 [IAU / Wikipedia]
- Located in the northern celestial hemisphere; spans right ascension 23h 25m to 02h 39m, declination −21.7° to +53.2° [Wikipedia]
- Visible from latitudes +90° to −40°; best placed for observation in November at 21:00 local time [Wikipedia]
- Bordering constellations: Perseus, Cassiopeia, Lacerta, Pegasus, Pisces, and Triangulum [Wikipedia]

### Notable stars
- **Mirach (β And):** Shared title of brightest star (magnitude 2.05–2.06); red giant, spectral type M0 III; ~197 light-years distant; used as a navigation star for locating M31 and M33 [Wikipedia, star-facts.com]
- **Alpheratz (α And):** Also magnitude ~2.06–2.07; formerly shared with Pegasus (was δ Pegasi); blue-white subgiant, spectral type B8IV–VHgMn, indicating a chemically peculiar mercury–manganese star; binary system; ~97 light-years distant; one corner of the Great Square of Pegasus asterism [Wikipedia, Britannica]
- **Almach (γ And):** Apparent magnitude 2.10–2.14; a beautiful multiple star system (four components); primary is an orange K-type bright giant (K2+IIb), contrasting with a blue-white companion (B9.5V) 9.6 arcseconds away — a showpiece telescopic double; ~350–390 light-years distant [Wikipedia (Almach)]
- **Delta Andromedae (δ And):** Magnitude 3.3; orange giant, spectral type K3; ~105 light-years [Wikipedia]
- **Ross 248:** A red dwarf, only ~10.3 light-years away, making it the ninth-closest star to the Sun; not visible to the naked eye [Wikipedia]
- **R Andromedae:** A Mira-type long-period variable red giant; brightness ranges from magnitude 5.8 to 14.9 over a period of ~409 days [Wikipedia]

### The Andromeda Galaxy (M31)
- The constellation's defining deep-sky object; the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way at ~2.5 million light-years (765 kpc) [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Apparent magnitude 3.44; one of the brightest Messier objects and the most distant object visible to the naked eye under dark skies [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Angular extent: 3.167° × 1°, making it about six times wider than the full Moon — though only the bright core is apparent to the unaided eye [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Diameter ~152,000 light-years; contains roughly 1 trillion stars; mass approximately 8×10¹¹ to 1.1×10¹² solar masses, comparable to the Milky Way [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Barred spiral galaxy classification; about 25% more luminous than the Milky Way [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Harbours a supermassive black hole (M31*) estimated at 1.1–2.3 × 10⁸ solar masses [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Contains ~460 globular clusters, including Mayall II — more luminous than any other known globular cluster in the Local Group [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]
- Currently approaching the Milky Way; ~50% probability of merging within ~10 billion years [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]

### M31's companion galaxies (also in Andromeda)
- **M32 (NGC 221):** Compact elliptical galaxy; apparent magnitude 9.0; discovered in 1749 by Le Gentil [Wikipedia]
- **M110 (NGC 205):** Dwarf elliptical satellite; apparent magnitude 8.9 [Wikipedia]

### Other deep-sky objects
- **NGC 891:** Edge-on barred spiral galaxy; magnitude 9.9; ~30 million light-years distant; noted for its prominent dust lane [Wikipedia]
- **NGC 752:** Open star cluster; magnitude 5.7 — one of the few open clusters easily resolved in binoculars [Wikipedia]
- **NGC 7686:** Open cluster; magnitude 5.6 [Wikipedia]
- **NGC 7662 (Blue Snowball Nebula):** Planetary nebula; magnitude 9.2; ~4,000 light-years away; shows a blue-green disc in a small telescope [Wikipedia]

### The Andromedids meteor shower
- Radiates from the constellation; associated with the debris trail of comet 3D/Biela [Wikipedia]
- Normal annual rate: fewer than 2 meteors per hour
- Historical outbursts of extraordinary intensity: 1872 and 1885 storms reached ~2 meteors per second; 2011 outburst produced ~50 meteors per hour [Wikipedia]

## Historical Context

- Andromeda is one of the 48 constellations listed by the Greek astronomer Ptolemy in his 2nd-century CE Almagest, and persists as one of the 88 modern IAU-recognised constellations [Wikipedia]
- The constellation represents the mythological princess Andromeda, daughter of Ethiopian king Cepheus and queen Cassiopeia. After Cassiopeia boasted that her daughter surpassed the sea nymphs (Nereids) in beauty, the god Poseidon sent the sea monster Cetus to devastate the kingdom. Andromeda was chained to a coastal rock as a sacrifice; Perseus, returning from slaying Medusa, killed the monster and married Andromeda [Wikipedia, Britannica]
- The surrounding family of constellations — Perseus, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, Cetus, Pegasus — share this mythological cycle
- The star Alpheratz was historically counted as belonging to both Andromeda and Pegasus (as δ Pegasi); in 1930 the IAU fixed constellation boundaries, assigning it definitively to Andromeda [Wikipedia]
- In the early 19th century, the French astronomer Le Monnier proposed a constellation called "Gloria Frederici" (Frederick's Glory) using stars now in northern Andromeda, honouring Frederick II of Prussia; this was never adopted and the stars reverted to Andromeda [Wikipedia, Britannica]
- The Andromeda Galaxy was first recorded by Persian astronomer Abd al-Rahman al-Sufi around 964 CE as a "little cloud"; telescopic observations began in the 17th century; Edwin Hubble definitively proved in 1925 that M31 is a separate galaxy far beyond the Milky Way using Cepheid variables [Wikipedia (Andromeda Galaxy)]

## Sub-types / Classifications

Not applicable — this is a single IAU constellation. Notable internal groupings:
- **Great Square of Pegasus:** The star Alpheratz (α And) forms the northeast corner of this prominent asterism spanning Andromeda and Pegasus
- **Frederick's Glory (obsolete):** A now-defunct proposed constellation using stars iota, kappa, lambda, omicron, and psi Andromedae [Wikipedia]
