## Definition
Submillimeter astronomy is the branch of observational astronomy conducted at wavelengths between roughly 0.1 mm (100 μm) and 1 mm, sitting between the far-infrared and microwave bands of the electromagnetic spectrum. These wavelengths reveal cold, dust-shrouded regions of the universe — star-forming clouds, protoplanetary disks, and distant starburst galaxies — that are largely invisible at optical wavelengths.

## Full Content

- Submillimeter astronomy operates at wavelengths from ~100 μm to 1 mm (frequencies ~300 GHz – 3 THz), positioned between far-infrared and radio astronomy; it shares techniques with both [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy; Astro4Edu glossary]
- These wavelengths are roughly 1,000 times longer than visible light [web orientation search]
- About half of all radiation emitted by stars and galaxies over cosmic history is absorbed by interstellar dust and re-emitted as thermal emission peaking in the far-infrared and submillimeter range [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- Cold objects — dust clouds, molecular cores at 10–50 K, protostars — emit most of their light in the submillimeter; submillimeter observations can see through optically thick dust clouds that are opaque at visible and UV wavelengths [Astro4Edu glossary; Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- A key advantage at high redshift: the "negative K-correction" means that as a dusty galaxy is observed at increasing redshifts, the redshifting of the thermal dust peak into the observing band roughly compensates for the increased distance, keeping fluxes relatively constant out to z ~ 6 [ScienceDirect: SMG review]

**Science applications:**
- **Molecular line emission**: CO rotational lines are the primary tracer of molecular hydrogen and star-forming gas; CI, CII, H₂O, HCN also detected [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **Dust continuum emission**: Maps thermal emission of dust, tracing column density and temperature in star-forming regions and protoplanetary disks [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **Protoplanetary disks**: ALMA resolved rings and gaps in disks around young stars (e.g. HL Tauri in 2014), directly revealing planet-forming structures [Wikipedia: ALMA; ESO: eso1436]
- **Submillimeter galaxies (SMGs)**: Dust-enshrouded starburst galaxies at high redshift (z ~ 1–6) with star formation rates exceeding hundreds to thousands of M☉/yr and infrared luminosities ≳ 10¹² L☉; discovered by SCUBA surveys at JCMT in 1997–1998 [web search: SMG discovery]
- **Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect**: Submillimeter observations measure the distortion of CMB photons by hot gas in galaxy clusters, used to find and study clusters at high redshift [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **Planetary science**: Thermal emission from planetary surfaces, cometary gas and dust; JCMT detected phosphine in Venus's cloud decks in 2020 [Wikipedia: JCMT]
- **Black hole imaging**: The Event Horizon Telescope used submillimeter VLBI at 1.3 mm / 870 μm to produce the first direct images of black holes — M87* (2019) and Sgr A* (2022) [Wikipedia: EHT]

**Atmospheric challenge:**
- Water vapor is the dominant absorber of submillimeter radiation; even a few millimetres of precipitable water vapor (PWV) blocks observations [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- Ground-based observations are limited to narrow atmospheric "windows" between water-vapor absorption bands: ~350, 450, 850 μm and ~1.1–1.3 mm [arXiv:1602.08795]
- Sites must be at high altitude and in extremely dry conditions; PWV below ~1 mm is optimal [arXiv:1602.08795]
- Best sites: Chajnantor plateau (Chile, 5,000 m), Mauna Kea (Hawaii, ~4,200 m), South Pole, Dome A/C (Antarctica), Hanle (India, ~4,500 m) [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- Space missions and balloon experiments avoid all atmospheric absorption [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]

**Key observatories:**
- **JCMT** (James Clerk Maxwell Telescope): 15 m single-dish on Mauna Kea; operational 1987; SCUBA camera (1997) found the first SMGs; SCUBA-2 (5,120 pixels at 450 and 850 μm, commissioned 2011); now operated by East Asian Observatory [Wikipedia: JCMT]
- **ALMA** (Atacama Large Millimeter/submillimeter Array): 66 antennas at 5,000 m on Chajnantor; 0.32–3.6 mm wavelength coverage; baselines 150 m – 14 km; 10 mas resolution; ~US$1.5 billion; science began 2011 [Wikipedia: ALMA]
- **SMA** (Submillimeter Array): Eight 6 m antennas on Mauna Kea; operated by CfA and ASIAA [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **APEX** (Atacama Pathfinder Experiment): 12 m single-dish on Chajnantor; operated by ESO/MPIfR/OSO [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **Herschel Space Observatory** (ESA, 2009–2013): 3.5 m mirror (largest space telescope at launch); covered 55–672 μm; instruments PACS, SPIRE, HIFI; operated from L2 [Wikipedia: Herschel]
- **EHT** (Event Horizon Telescope): Global submillimeter VLBI network; 870 μm since 2018; ~19 μas resolution; imaged M87* and Sgr A* [Wikipedia: EHT]
- **CSO** (Caltech Submillimeter Observatory): Historic 10.4 m dish on Mauna Kea; first light 1986; first submillimeter interferometer (with JCMT); now decommissioned [CSO History, Caltech]

## Historical Context

- **Late 1960s**: UK Science Research Council recognized the scientific potential of submillimeter wavelengths; planning began for a dedicated submillimeter telescope [Wikipedia: JCMT]
- **1973**: Robert Leighton (Caltech) proposed the Caltech Submillimeter Observatory; construction started 1984 in Pasadena, then shipped to Mauna Kea [CSO History, Caltech]
- **1975**: UK SRC committee concluded a 15 m telescope could observe at wavelengths down to 750–800 μm — the specification that became JCMT [Wikipedia: JCMT]
- **1986**: CSO achieved first light with a spectrum of the CO J=2–1 line from starburst galaxy M82 — the first dedicated submillimeter telescope science result [CSO History, Caltech]
- **1987**: Both JCMT and CSO began formal operations on Mauna Kea; together they formed the first submillimeter interferometer [Wikipedia: JCMT; CSO History]
- **1997**: SCUBA camera commissioned on JCMT; Smail, Ivison & Blain (1997) discovered submillimeter galaxies using SCUBA at 850 μm, revealing an unexpected population of dust-obscured high-redshift starbursts [web search: SMG discovery]
- **1998**: Hughes et al. published submillimeter observations of the Hubble Deep Field, confirming a large population of high-z submillimeter galaxies; results changed understanding of the cosmic star formation history [web search: SMG discovery]
- **1998**: SWAS (NASA) became the first dedicated space-based submillimeter observatory [Wikipedia: Submillimetre astronomy]
- **2009**: ESA launched Herschel Space Observatory (3.5 m mirror), the largest infrared/submillimeter space telescope; operated until 2013 when liquid helium coolant was exhausted [Wikipedia: Herschel]
- **2011**: ALMA began early science; SCUBA-2 commissioned on JCMT [Wikipedia: ALMA; JCMT]
- **2013**: ALMA reached full operational capability [Wikipedia: ALMA]
- **2019**: EHT produced the first direct image of a black hole (M87*) at 1.3 mm wavelength [Wikipedia: EHT]
- **2022**: EHT imaged the Milky Way's central black hole, Sgr A*, at 870 μm [Wikipedia: EHT]

The field name derives from the sub-millimetre (or submillimeter) range: wavelengths shorter than 1 mm but longer than the far-infrared. The spelling varies by national convention (submillimetre in British English, submillimeter in American English).

## Sub-types / Classifications

1. **Single-dish continuum surveys**: Map large sky areas in thermal dust emission (e.g. SCUBA/SCUBA-2 at 850 μm); best for wide-field source discovery and extended emission
2. **Interferometric imaging**: Arrays (ALMA, SMA) combine antennas for high angular resolution (down to 10 mas with ALMA); essential for resolving protoplanetary disks, galaxy nuclei, and compact sources
3. **Spectral line observations**: Heterodyne receivers detect narrow molecular and atomic emission lines (CO, CI, CII, H₂O) with high spectral resolution; traces gas kinematics, chemistry, and redshifts
4. **Space-based submillimeter missions**: Satellites (Herschel, SWAS, Planck) avoid atmospheric absorption entirely, surveying the full 55–1000 μm range across the whole sky
5. **Balloon-borne platforms**: Experiments like BLAST carry submillimeter cameras above most of the atmosphere for wide-field surveys without orbital costs
6. **Submillimeter VLBI**: The EHT links submillimeter observatories globally into an Earth-sized interferometer achieving microarcsecond angular resolution for imaging black hole shadows
