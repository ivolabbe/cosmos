# Earth — Article Spec

## Category
solar-system

## Definition
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only astronomical object known to harbour life, distinguished by its liquid surface water, oxygen-rich atmosphere, active plate tectonics, and global magnetic field. It formed approximately 4.54 billion years ago and orbits the Sun at a mean distance of 1 AU (149,598,023 km).

## Full Content

### Physical Properties
- Mass: 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg — densest planet in the Solar System [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Mean radius: 6,371 km; equatorial radius 6,378 km; polar radius 6,357 km (oblate spheroid due to rotation) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Equatorial diameter: 12,756 km (7,926 miles) [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Mean density: 5.513 g/cm³ — highest of any planet [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Surface gravity: 9.807 m/s² (1 g) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Escape velocity: 11.186 km/s [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Equatorial rotation velocity: 1,674.4 km/h [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Rotation period (sidereal): 23 h 56 m 4.1 s (0.99727 days) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Surface temperature: mean 14.76°C; range −89.2°C to 56.7°C; blackbody temperature 255 K [Wikipedia/Earth]

### Orbital Parameters
- Semi-major axis: 149,598,023 km (1 AU) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Orbital period: 365.256363 days [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Average orbital speed: 29.783 km/s [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Orbital eccentricity: 0.0167 (nearly circular) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Aphelion: 152,097,597 km; Perihelion: 147,098,450 km [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Axial tilt: 23.4393° — responsible for Earth's seasons [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Number of natural satellites: 1 (the Moon) [NASA Science/Earth Facts]

### Atmosphere
- Composition by volume (dry air): nitrogen 78.08%, oxygen 20.95%, argon 0.934%, carbon dioxide 0.0415%, neon 0.00182%, helium 0.00052%, methane 0.00017% [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Water vapour: variable, up to ~1% [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Surface pressure: 101.325 kPa (1 atm) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Five main layers: troposphere (0–12 km), stratosphere (12–50 km, contains ozone layer), mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Atmosphere shields the surface from harmful UV radiation and prevents most meteoroids from reaching the ground [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Ozone layer in stratosphere absorbs 97–99% of incoming solar UV radiation [Britannica/Earth]

### Internal Structure
- Crust: 0–35 km, density 2.2–2.9 g/cm³; continental crust ~30 km thick, oceanic crust ~5 km [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Upper mantle: 35–660 km, density 3.4–4.4 g/cm³ [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Lower mantle: 660–2,890 km, density 3.4–5.6 g/cm³ [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Outer core: 2,890–5,100 km, liquid iron-nickel, density 9.9–12.2 g/cm³ [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Inner core: 5,100–6,378 km, solid iron-nickel, density 12.8–13.1 g/cm³; temperature up to 6,000°C, pressure ~360 GPa [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Bulk composition by mass: iron 32.1%, oxygen 30.1%, silicon 15.1%, magnesium 13.9%, sulfur 2.9%, nickel 1.8%, calcium 1.5%, aluminium 1.4% [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Mean heat loss from interior: 87 mW/m² (global total 4.42 × 10¹³ W); driven by radioactive decay and primordial heat [Wikipedia/Earth]

### Magnetic Field
- Generated by convection in the liquid outer core (geodynamo) [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Magnetic dipole moment: 7.79 × 10²² A·m² [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Field strength at magnetic equator: 3.05 × 10⁻⁵ Tesla [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Magnetosphere extends ~10 Earth radii on the day side [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Protects the surface from solar wind and cosmic rays; creates auroras at high latitudes [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Field has weakened ~6% per century in recent times; most recent polarity reversal ~700,000 years ago [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Geomagnetic storms occur when solar wind disturbs the magnetosphere [COSMOS article: geomagnetic-storms]

### Hydrosphere and Unique Features
- Oceans cover 70.8% of Earth's surface (361 million km²); land covers 29.2% (149 million km²) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Average ocean depth: ~3.6 km [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- 97% of Earth's total water is in the oceans; remainder in ice sheets, glaciers, groundwater [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Only planet in the Solar System with stable liquid water on the surface — a prerequisite for known life [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Seven continents: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia/Oceania, Europe, North America, South America [Britannica/Earth]

### Plate Tectonics
- Lithosphere (crust + rigid upper mantle, ~60 km thick) divided into ~15 major tectonic plates moving at 2–15 cm/yr [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Plate interactions produce mountain ranges (e.g. Himalayas), ocean trenches, volcanic arcs, and earthquakes [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Earth is the only planet in the Solar System known to have active plate tectonics [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Subduction recycles oceanic crust back into the mantle; continental crust is older and less dense [Wikipedia/Earth]

### Biosphere
- Only astronomical object known to harbour life [Wikipedia/Earth; NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Life arose approximately 3.8 billion years ago [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Biosphere spans from ocean floor vents to the upper atmosphere [Britannica/Earth]
- Photosynthesis by early cyanobacteria produced Earth's free oxygen atmosphere ~2.4 billion years ago (Great Oxidation Event) [Wikipedia/Earth]

### The Moon
- Earth's sole natural satellite; fifth largest moon in the Solar System [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Lunar radius: 1,738 km (roughly one-quarter of Earth's) [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Mean Earth–Moon distance: 384,400 km (238,855 miles) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Moon's gravity stabilises Earth's axial tilt, moderating long-term climate variability [Wikipedia/Earth; NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Responsible for ocean tides and is gradually slowing Earth's rotation [Wikipedia/Earth]

### Observation from Space
- First photographed from space during Apollo 17 (December 7, 1972) — the "Blue Marble" image [NASA/Blue Marble]
- Appears distinctly blue due to oceans and white cloud cover; ice caps visible at poles [Britannica/Earth]
- Located in the Orion–Cygnus Arm of the Milky Way, ~26,000 light-years from the galactic centre [Britannica/Earth]
- Name does not derive from Greco-Roman mythology; comes from Old English/Germanic words meaning "ground" [NASA Science/Earth Facts; Wikipedia/Earth]

## Historical Context
- Earth formed ~4.54 billion years ago from the solar nebula via accretion [Wikipedia/Earth; Britannica/Earth]
- Initially lacked free oxygen, oceans, and an ozone layer; extremely hot surface [Britannica/Earth]
- Moon likely formed from a giant impact (~4.5 Ga) between proto-Earth and a Mars-sized body (Theia) [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Oceans formed within the first billion years; life arose ~3.8 Ga [NASA Science/Earth Facts]
- Cyanobacteria oxygenated the atmosphere ~2.4 Ga, enabling complex life [Wikipedia/Earth]
- Modern plate tectonics has shaped continents and climate over hundreds of millions of years [Wikipedia/Earth]

## Handoff: Researcher → Writer

**Confidence:** High. All key facts are sourced to Wikipedia, NASA Science, or Britannica. Numbers are consistent across sources.

**Recommended article length:** 500–700 words (rich topic with many dimensions).

**Hero image:** `earth-blue-marble.jpg` — iconic, high resolution, immediately recognisable.

**Key angle for writer:** Lead with Earth's uniqueness — the only planet known to harbour life — then ground readers in the physics that make this possible: liquid water, oxygen atmosphere, plate tectonics, magnetic shield. Avoid dry list-of-facts tone; frame each feature as part of a connected system.

**Watch for:** 
- "Data" are plural throughout.
- Distinguish sidereal rotation period (23h 56m) from solar day (24h).
- Internal structure depths vary slightly by source — use Wikipedia values (derived from seismology) as authoritative.
- Do not conflate Earth's age (4.54 Ga) with life's origin (~3.8 Ga).
