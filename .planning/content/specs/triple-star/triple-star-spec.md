## Definition
A triple star (or triple star system) is a gravitationally bound system of three stars that orbit their common centre of mass. Most triple systems are hierarchical: a close inner binary is orbited by a more distant third star, an arrangement that confers long-term dynamical stability.

## Full Content

### What is a triple star?

- A triple star is a gravitationally bound system of three stars [NED/IPAC glossary]
- All three stars orbit the system's common centre of mass (barycentre) [Wikipedia: Star system]
- The overwhelming majority of stable triple systems are **hierarchical**: one close inner binary pair plus a third star orbiting the binary at a much greater distance [Wikipedia; arXiv:2504.02939]
- The stability condition requires the outer orbit to be at least ~7.5 times larger than the inner binary orbit: a_out/a_in ≳ 7.5 [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Non-hierarchical ("trapezium") configurations are dynamically unstable and typically eject one star, leaving a stable binary [Wikipedia: Star system]
- Terms "triple," "ternary," and "trinary" are used interchangeably [Wikipedia: Star system]

### How common are triple stars?

- Roughly half of all stars are single; about a third of stellar systems are binary; triples account for approximately one in twelve stellar systems [Wikipedia: Star system]
- Frequency strongly depends on stellar mass: >50% of O/B-type stars have triple or higher-order configurations, whereas ~10–15% of solar-type (FGK) stars do [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Short-period binaries (P < 3 days) among FGK stars show triple fractions >95%, suggesting the outer companion plays a key role in shrinking the inner binary [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Observed frequencies are likely underestimates due to detection bias against faint, close companions [arXiv:2504.02939]
- In Tokovinin's (1999) catalogue of multiple stars, 551 of 728 systems described are triple [Wikipedia: Star system]

### Orbital dynamics and stability

- The von Zeipel–Lidov–Kozai (vZLK) mechanism is the primary dynamical driver in high-inclination hierarchical triples (inclination 40°–140°): it causes cyclic exchange between orbital eccentricity and inclination on long secular timescales [arXiv:2504.02939]
- The conserved Kozai constant: √(1 − e²) cos(i) = constant [arXiv:2504.02939]
- vZLK oscillations coupled with tidal friction can gradually shrink the inner binary — a pathway that produces many short-period contact binaries [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Four dynamical regimes: (1) hierarchical/stable, (2) secular/vZLK, (3) quasi-secular (chaotic, a_out/a_in ≈ 3–7.5), (4) non-hierarchical (disruption) [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Retrograde planetary orbits in triple systems are ~30–37% more stable than prograde orbits [A&A 2018]

### Formation

- Triple systems form in star-forming molecular clouds through gravitational fragmentation and capture during the early chaotic phase of cluster evolution [arXiv:2504.02939; Wikipedia]
- Wide multiple systems in young stellar populations show evidence that hierarchical collapse is a dominant formation mechanism [ADS/Duquennoy & Mayor 1991; Duchêne & Kraus 2013]
- The high triple fraction among short-period binaries suggests outer companions drive inner binary hardening through secular dynamics [arXiv:2504.02939]

### Evolutionary outcomes

- Triple systems produce outcomes impossible through isolated binary evolution alone [arXiv:2504.02939]
- Outcomes include: short-period interacting binaries, stellar mergers, blue straggler stars, compact object binaries (neutron star/white dwarf pairs), gravitational wave sources [arXiv:2504.02939]
- TIC 290061484's inner pair will merge and trigger a supernova in approximately 20–40 million years [NASA TESS press release 2024]

### Planets in triple systems

- 32 planets are known in triple star systems (as of the 2018 A&A study) [A&A 2018]
- Most (29) are in S3 orbits — circling only the outer, tertiary star [A&A 2018]
- Planets can also be in S1/S2 orbits (around one star of the inner binary) or P1 orbits (circumbinary around the inner pair) [A&A 2018]
- No circumtriple (P2) planet has been confirmed [A&A 2018]
- Planet KOI-5Ab (~0.5 Saturn masses) orbits the primary of the KOI-5 triple system every 5 days; its orbital plane is tilted 50° relative to the inner binary plane, likely due to gravitational interaction with the companion [NASA Kepler]
- Alpha Centauri C (Proxima) hosts at least one planet (Proxima Centauri b) in the triple system nearest to the Sun [NASA Science]
- Gliese 667 (a triple system) has a third component that may host 2–7 habitable-zone planets [Wikipedia]

## Historical Context

- Multiple stars have been recognised since antiquity; Galileo Galilei observed double stars in the early 1600s
- William Herschel systematically catalogued double and multiple stars from the 1780s, discovering Polaris B in August 1779 [Wikipedia: Polaris]
- The inner companion of Polaris (Ab) was not directly detected until radial velocity studies in the early 20th century; Hubble Space Telescope images confirmed all three components in 2006 [Wikipedia: Polaris]
- Alpha Centauri's triple nature (Proxima Centauri as third member) was established only with precise proper motion measurements in the 20th century; Proxima's gravitational binding was debated until the 2010s
- Aleksandr Tokovinin compiled the Multiple Star Catalogue (MSC), the definitive modern reference; his 1999 version lists 728 systems [Wikipedia: Star system]
- The KOI-5 triple star system's planet was originally flagged as a candidate by NASA's Kepler mission in 2009 but set aside; confirmed in 2021 when Palomar and Keck data resolved the triple nature [NASA Kepler press release]
- TIC 290061484 broke the record for the shortest outer orbital period (25 days, beating the 33-day record held since 1956), discovered using TESS + citizen science + machine learning (Kostov et al. 2024, ApJ) [NASA 2024]

## Sub-types / Classifications

**By orbital architecture:**
- **Hierarchical triple**: Stable configuration; inner binary + distant third component (a_out/a_in ≳ 7.5). Vast majority of observed systems.
- **Compact hierarchical triple (CHT)**: All three stars within a very small volume; outer period can be days to weeks (e.g. TIC 290061484: 25-day outer period).
- **Trapezium system**: Non-hierarchical; three or more stars with comparable orbital scales; dynamically unstable, found in young star clusters; typically dissolves into stable binary + ejected star.

**By stellar types of components** (not a formal classification, but observationally relevant):
- Triple systems span all combinations of stellar class (OB triples, solar-type, red dwarf triples)
- The vZLK mechanism is particularly important in triples with an inclined outer companion

**By planetary orbit type in triple systems:**
- S1/S2: planet around one star of inner binary
- S3: planet around the outer star only (most common among known triple-system planets)
- P1: circumbinary planet (planet orbits both inner stars)
- P2: circumtriple planet (orbits all three; none confirmed)
