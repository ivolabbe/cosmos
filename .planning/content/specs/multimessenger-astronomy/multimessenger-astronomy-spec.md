## Definition

Multimessenger astronomy is the coordinated observation and interpretation of multiple distinct types of signals — electromagnetic radiation, gravitational waves, neutrinos, and cosmic rays — from the same astronomical source or event, enabling a more complete physical picture than any single messenger can provide. [Wikipedia: Multi-messenger astronomy]

## Full Content

### The Four Messengers

- **Electromagnetic radiation** is the oldest and broadest messenger — spanning radio to gamma rays — but is absorbed, scattered, or delayed by intervening matter; alone it cannot reveal the interiors of dense objects. [Britannica]
- **Gravitational waves** are ripples in spacetime caused by accelerating masses; they pass through matter unimpeded, directly encoding the dynamics of compact binary mergers and collapsing cores. First directly detected by LIGO in 2015 (GW150914, a black hole merger). [Wikipedia; LIGO Caltech]
- **Neutrinos** are nearly massless, chargeless particles produced in nuclear reactions and particle acceleration; they escape the cores of supernovae and active galaxies where photons are trapped, arriving seconds to hours before or during optical emission. [Britannica; Wikipedia: SN 1987A]
- **Cosmic rays** are highly energetic charged particles (mostly protons and atomic nuclei); because they are deflected by magnetic fields they generally cannot be pointed back to their sources, but correlated detections with other messengers help identify origin sites. [Britannica; Wikipedia]

### Physical Principle

- Different messengers are produced by different physical processes and travel through the universe differently; combining them breaks degeneracies that confound single-messenger analyses. [Wikipedia]
- The absence of an expected messenger is itself informative: the lack of detected neutrinos from GW170817 (attributed to the jet being strongly off-axis) constrained the geometry of the merger. [Wikipedia]

### Key Event: GW170817 (August 17, 2017)

- LIGO and Virgo detected gravitational waves from a binary neutron star merger (GW170817) in the galaxy NGC 4993, ~130 million light-years away; neutron star masses were 1.1–1.6 M☉. [LIGO Caltech, 2017]
- The gravitational wave signal lasted ~100 seconds — roughly 500 times longer than black hole merger signals. [LIGO Caltech]
- Fermi-GBM detected gamma-ray burst GRB 170817A just 1.74 ± 0.05 s after the gravitational wave arrival. [arXiv:1710.05834]
- The time delay constrains the difference in speed between gravitational waves and light to better than 1 part in 10¹⁵, confirming both travel at effectively the same speed. [arXiv:1710.05834]
- Optical counterpart SSS17a (the kilonova) was identified ~11 hours later by ~70 optical astronomy partners; about one-quarter of the world's professional astronomers participated in follow-up. [LIGO Caltech; Wikipedia]
- The kilonova produced heavy r-process elements including gold and platinum, providing direct evidence for neutron star mergers as a major site of heavy element synthesis. [ESO; ESA/Hubble]
- GRB 170817A was 2–6 orders of magnitude less luminous than typical short gamma-ray bursts, consistent with an off-axis jet geometry. [arXiv:1710.05834]

### Key Event: IceCube-170922A / TXS 0506+056 (September 22, 2017)

- IceCube detected a ~290 TeV muon neutrino (IceCube-170922A) and within minutes issued automated alerts directing telescopes to a 1.33-degree sky region. [Wikipedia: TXS 0506+056]
- The source was identified as blazar TXS 0506+056, a BL Lac-type object at z = 0.3365 (~1.75 Gpc), which was in an active gamma-ray flaring state. [Wikipedia: TXS 0506+056]
- Fermi-LAT (>100 MeV) and MAGIC (100–400 GeV) detected correlated gamma-ray emission. [Wikipedia: Multi-messenger astronomy]
- This was the first time a neutrino detector localised an extragalactic object and the first identification of a cosmic-ray accelerator site outside our Galaxy (above solar/SN 1987A energies). [Wikipedia: TXS 0506+056]
- Archival IceCube data revealed a lower-energy neutrino excess from TXS 0506+056 in 2014–2015. [Wikipedia: TXS 0506+056]

### Key Event: SN 1987A (February 23, 1987)

- Supernova 1987A in the Large Magellanic Cloud (51.4 kpc away) produced a burst of ~25 antineutrinos detected across three observatories (Kamiokande-II: 12; IMB: 8; Baksan: 5) in a burst lasting under 13 seconds. [Wikipedia: SN 1987A]
- The neutrino burst arrived ~2–3 hours before the optical discovery, confirming theoretical predictions that ~99% of a core collapse's energy (~10⁴⁶ J) is radiated as neutrinos. [Wikipedia: SN 1987A; Wikipedia: Multi-messenger astronomy]
- This was the first confirmed extrasolar neutrino detection and established neutrino astronomy as a practical observational tool. [Wikipedia: Multi-messenger astronomy]
- The progenitor was identified as blue supergiant Sanduleak −69 202, unexpectedly revising stellar evolution theory. [Wikipedia: SN 1987A]

### Recent Developments (2019–2023)

- October 2019: A high-energy neutrino was associated with tidal disruption event AT2019dsg; a second was linked to AT2019fdr (November 2019). [Wikipedia]
- June 2023: IceCube used a cascade neutrino technique to detect diffuse neutrino emission from the galactic plane of the Milky Way for the first time, creating the first neutrino-based galactic map. [Wikipedia]

### Coordinating Infrastructure

- **SNEWS (Supernova Early Warning System)**: Founded 1999 at Brookhaven; automated since 2005; pools multiple neutrino detectors to provide supernova early warning with seconds of lead time. [Wikipedia]
- **AMON (Astrophysical Multimessenger Observatory Network)**: Founded 2013 at Pennsylvania State University; facilitates real-time sharing of sub-threshold events across observatories to identify coincidences no single detector could flag. [Wikipedia]
- **GCN (Gamma-ray Coordinates Network)**: Distributes rapid alerts for gamma-ray bursts and associated transients. [context from sources]

### Scientific Significance

- Multimessenger detections provide independent constraints on the Hubble constant (GW170817 yielded H₀ ≈ 70 km/s/Mpc, independently of the cosmic distance ladder). [Wikipedia; LIGO Caltech]
- Each messenger tests different physics: GW timing tests gravity; neutrino arrival times test neutrino mass; correlated EM emission tests jet physics and nucleosynthesis. [Britannica; arXiv:1710.05834]
- The field transforms catastrophic events from single-channel signals into multi-dimensional portraits. [Wikipedia]

## Historical Context

- **1930s–1940s**: Radio astronomy (Jansky 1932; Reber 1940s) expanded the electromagnetic window beyond visible light; cosmic rays were identified from solar flares. [Wikipedia]
- **1960s**: Neutrino detectors began operating (Raymond Davis Jr.'s Homestake experiment, 1967), detecting solar neutrinos and revealing the solar neutrino problem — a deficit later explained by neutrino oscillations, which require non-zero mass. [Britannica]
- **February 23, 1987**: SN 1987A neutrinos detected hours before optical light — the founding multimessenger event; established neutrino astronomy as practical. [Wikipedia: SN 1987A]
- **1999**: SNEWS founded, formalising coordinated multi-detector neutrino monitoring. [Wikipedia]
- **2013**: AMON founded to coordinate all-sky sub-threshold alert sharing. [Wikipedia]
- **September 14, 2015**: LIGO makes the first direct detection of gravitational waves (GW150914), a binary black hole merger; opens the gravitational wave messenger window. [Wikipedia; LIGO Caltech]
- **August 17, 2017**: GW170817 — the first binary neutron star merger in gravitational waves, and the first event observed in both gravitational waves and light; widely considered the birth of the modern multimessenger era. [LIGO Caltech; arXiv:1710.05834]
- **September 22, 2017**: IceCube-170922A correlated with blazar TXS 0506+056 — first extragalactic neutrino point source. [Wikipedia: TXS 0506+056]
- **June 2023**: IceCube detects diffuse neutrino emission from the Milky Way's galactic plane — first neutrino map of our Galaxy. [Wikipedia]

## Sub-types / Classifications

Multimessenger astronomy is typically organised by the messenger pairs or combinations involved:

| Messenger combination | Example event | Key observable |
|----------------------|---------------|---------------|
| GW + gamma-ray (EM) | GW170817 + GRB 170817A | Neutron star merger; short GRB progenitor confirmed |
| GW + optical/IR/radio (EM) | GW170817 + kilonova SSS17a | r-process nucleosynthesis; Hubble constant |
| Neutrino + gamma-ray (EM) | IceCube-170922A + TXS 0506+056 | Blazar as cosmic-ray accelerator |
| Neutrino + optical (EM) | SN 1987A | Core-collapse supernova dynamics |
| Neutrino + diffuse EM | Milky Way galactic plane 2023 | Galactic cosmic-ray production |
| Cosmic ray + gamma-ray | Ongoing (Pierre Auger + Fermi) | Ultra-high-energy cosmic-ray sources |

Non-detection of an expected messenger is also a multimessenger result: the absence of neutrinos from GW170817 constrains the jet geometry.
