**Caption:** Illustration of a Type Ia supernova mechanism. A white dwarf (the remnant of a dead star) orbits a companion star. The white dwarf's intense gravity pulls material from the companion, gradually accreting it onto the white dwarf's surface. When the white dwarf reaches approximately 1.4 solar masses (the Chandrasekhar limit), the pressure and temperature in its core become extreme enough to trigger uncontrolled nuclear fusion, resulting in a thermonuclear explosion that destroys the entire star.
