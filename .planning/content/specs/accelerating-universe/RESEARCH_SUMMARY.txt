KEY FACTS VERIFIED:
===================

Discovery Timeline:
- January 1998: Perlmutter announces SCP findings
- May 1998: Riess & High-Z team publish independent confirmation (first formal publication)
- December 1998: Both teams named "Breakthrough of the Year" by Science magazine
- 2011: Nobel Prize in Physics awarded to Perlmutter, Riess, Schmidt

Teams & People:
- Supernova Cosmology Project: Led by Saul Perlmutter (Lawrence Berkeley Lab)
- High-Z Supernova Search Team: Led by Brian P. Schmidt (started 1994), with Adam G. Riess as key contributor
- Both teams used Type Ia supernovae as standard candles

Key Numbers:
- Dark energy fraction: ~68% of universe composition
- Ordinary matter: ~5%
- Dark matter: ~27%
- Hubble constant (CMB): ~67 km/s/Mpc
- Hubble constant (distance ladder): ~72-74 km/s/Mpc
- SN 1997ff distance: ~10 billion light-years (z~1.7)
- Equation of state parameter: w = -1 (cosmological constant)
- Chandrasekhar limit (Type Ia progenitor): ~1.4 solar masses

Mechanism Explained:
- Type Ia supernovae result from white dwarf + companion binary systems
- White dwarf accretes material until reaching Chandrasekhar limit
- Thermonuclear runaway triggers explosion
- Nearly constant peak brightness makes them "standard candles"
- Dimmer-than-expected distant supernovae indicated acceleration

Dark Energy Explanations:
- Leading candidate: Cosmological constant (Λ) — vacuum energy with w = -1
- Alternative models: Quintessence (w > -1), modified gravity
- Current observations favor cosmological constant

Future of Universe:
- Most likely: Big Freeze (heat death) with continued acceleration
- Alternative: Big Rip (if w < -1, but currently disfavored)
