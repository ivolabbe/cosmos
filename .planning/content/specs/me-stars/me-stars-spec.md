## Definition

Me stars are stars of spectral type M — cool, red objects with surface temperatures below ~3,900 K — that additionally display emission lines in their spectra, indicated by the "e" suffix appended to the MK spectral classification. The designation spans two physically distinct populations: magnetically active M dwarfs (red dwarfs showing chromospheric hydrogen emission, often written dMe) and pulsating M giant variables (such as Mira variables, classified as M IIIe) whose emission lines arise from shock waves propagating through their extended atmospheres.

## Full Content

### The "e" spectral suffix
- The lowercase "e" appended to a spectral type indicates emission lines are present in the spectrum [Wikipedia: Stellar classification]
- The full notation "Me" means: M spectral type + emission lines; "dMe" adds the "d" (dwarf) prefix for main-sequence red dwarfs [handprint.com specclass]
- Related suffixes: "er" (emission with central reversal), "eq" (emission with P Cygni profile); these appear in active M stars too [Wikipedia: Stellar classification]
- The "e" designation was formalized within the MK (Morgan–Keenan–Kellman) classification system, introduced in 1943 [Wikipedia: Stellar classification]

### Me dwarfs (dMe stars)
- dMe stars are main-sequence M dwarfs whose chromospheres are active enough to produce H-alpha (6563 Å) and other Balmer lines in emission, rather than absorption [ADS: Stauffer & Hartmann 1986]
- The physical driver is magnetic activity: rapid rotation induces a strong dynamo in the fully convective interior, heating the chromosphere and corona to emit UV and X-rays alongside optical emission lines [Wikipedia: Stellar classification; astro activity/age reviews]
- As chromospheric activity increases from inactive M dwarfs, H-alpha first deepens as an absorption line, then fills in, then goes into emission — the most active stars are the dMe class [Cram & Mullan 1979, cited in activity/age review]
- The Balmer lines (especially H-alpha) are the dominant chromospheric radiative coolants in dMe stars; Ca II H & K emission is also commonly observed [CARMENES A&A 2018]
- The fraction of M dwarfs that are emission-line (dMe) rises steeply with decreasing mass: ~5% at M0, ~50% at M4.5, ~100% at M5.5 and later [handprint.com specclass]
- Late-type dMe stars (M5+) can sustain activity for >7 Gyr; early-type dMe stars (M0–M2) spin down and become inactive within 1–2 Gyr [activity/age review ADS]
- dMe stars are systematically younger, kinematically hot, and slightly bluer (in B–V) for their luminosity than inactive dM counterparts [Stauffer & Hartmann 1986]
- Proxima Centauri (M5.5Ve, 4.24 ly) is the closest example: ~88% surface activity fraction, a 2019 flare of far-UV energy ~2×10³⁰ erg [Wikipedia: Proxima Centauri]
- EV Lacertae (M3.5Ve, 16.5 ly): age ~300 Myr, recorded 2008 flare thousands of times more powerful than the Sun's largest solar flare [Wikipedia: EV Lacertae]
- AD Leonis (M3.5Ve, 16.2 ly): ~73% active surface area, surface magnetic flux ~3 kG, 2021 superflare detected in both X-ray and optical [Wikipedia: AD Leonis]
- UV Ceti (Gliese 65 B, M5.5Ve, 8.77 ly): a defining prototype; brightness can increase 75× in 20 seconds; prototype of the UV Ceti-type variable class [Wikipedia: Gliese 65]
- YY Geminorum (= Castor C): dM1e + dM1e eclipsing binary; all red dwarfs in the Castor system have emission lines and are flare stars [Wikipedia: YY Geminorum]
- dMe stars are also classified as UV Ceti-type variables (eruptive variable stars) and frequently as BY Draconis variables (starspot-induced variability) [daviddarling.info: UV Ceti star]

### Me giants (M IIIe and M Ie stars)
- M giant Me stars include Mira variables and other long-period variables (LPVs); shock waves driven by pulsation propagate through the stellar atmosphere and produce emission lines [NED/Gray: Emission Lines in Mira Variables]
- Emission lines strengthen near light maximum; hydrogen Balmer lines and metal lines (Fe I 4202, 4308 Å; Mg I 4571 Å) are observed [NED/Gray; web search compilation]
- The hydrogen and metal emission lines are in antiphase with each other (H-alpha strengthens when Fe I/Mg I weaken, and vice versa) [NED/Gray]
- Mira (o Ceti, M7 IIIe): the prototype Mira variable, spectral type varying M5e–M9e as it pulsates; period ~332 days; luminosity 8,400–9,360 L☉; magnitude range 2.0–10.1 [Wikipedia: Mira]
- Mira variables are broadly classified as Me, Ce (carbon-rich), or Se (S-type, carbon/oxygen equal) depending on chemistry [web search compilation]
- At light minimum, TiO molecular bands dominate the M giant spectrum and weaken emission; near maximum, shock propagation re-excites emission [NED/Gray]
- Natural masers (OH, SiO, H₂O) also form in the cool extended atmospheres of AGB Me stars [Wikipedia: Mira variable]

### Spectral characteristics distinguishing dMe from dM
- Standard M-type spectra show strong TiO bands (6322, 6569, 6651, 7053, 7666, 8206, 8432 Å), CaH bands, and neutral metal absorption [web search compilation]
- In dMe stars, H-alpha (6563 Å) appears in emission; at the most active extreme, higher Balmer lines (Hβ, Hγ, Hδ) also emit [CARMENES A&A 2018; NED/Gray]
- Ca II H & K emission (393, 397 nm) correlates positively with H-alpha activity in dM stars, except during flare events [CARMENES A&A 2023]
- X-ray and UV emission accompany optical H-alpha emission in active dMe stars, signalling a heated corona [Osten et al. 2006 ApJ 647:1349]
- VO bands (7334, 7851 Å) appear in very late M dwarfs (M7+); these ultra-cool dMe stars can include brown dwarf boundary objects [web search compilation]

## Historical Context

- Angelo Secchi introduced spectral classes for "red" stars (Type III) in 1866; he later added a Type V for emission-line stars — the conceptual precursor to "Me" notation [web search compilation / Secchi history]
- The Harvard Spectral Classification (late 19th–early 20th century), developed by Annie Jump Cannon and the Harvard "computers," established the O–B–A–F–G–K–M sequence used today [Wikipedia: Stellar classification]
- The Morgan–Keenan–Kellman (MK) system (1943) added luminosity classes and formalized special suffix notation including "e" for emission lines [Wikipedia: Stellar classification]
- David Fabricius first recorded the variability of Mira (o Ceti) on 3 August 1596; Johannes Holwarda determined its period (~1638); Johannes Hevelius named it "Mira" (Latin: wonderful) in 1662 — one of the first Me giants recognized [Wikipedia: Mira]
- UV Ceti-type flare stars were recognized as a distinct class in the mid-20th century; their dMe classification consolidated the link between emission-line M dwarfs and eruptive activity [AAVSO: UV Ceti and the flare stars]
- The systematic study of dMe fractions as a function of spectral subtype was quantified in the late 1980s–1990s, revealing the steep dependence on stellar mass and age [Stauffer & Hartmann 1986 ApJS 61:531; Gizis et al. 2002]

## Sub-types / Classifications

| Sub-type | Luminosity class | Typical examples | Distinguishing feature |
|----------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| **dMe** | V (dwarf / main sequence) | Proxima Cen (M5.5Ve), EV Lac (M3.5Ve), UV Cet (M5.5Ve), AD Leo (M3.5Ve), YY Gem (dM1e+dM1e) | H-alpha (+ Balmer) emission from chromospheric magnetic activity; UV/X-ray active; flare stars |
| **Me giant** (M IIIe) | III (giant) | Mira / o Ceti (M7 IIIe), R Leo, S Leo | Emission from pulsation shock waves; Balmer + metal lines; Mira variables |
| **Me supergiant** (M Ie) | I (supergiant) | Some red supergiants (e.g., VX Sgr) | Extended stellar wind; P Cygni profiles possible |
| **dMe / UV Ceti type** | V | UV Ceti, EV Lacertae, AD Leonis | Active dMe that also shows eruptive flaring (UV Ceti variable class) |
| **dMe / BY Dra type** | V | YY Geminorum, many dMe stars | Spotted M dwarfs with rotational modulation, also emission-line |

Note: "Me" is a notation, not a class independent of luminosity class. The same "e" suffix applies across the HR diagram for M-type objects showing emission.

**Watch for:**
- "Me star" spans two very different objects (tiny active red dwarfs and giant pulsating Mira variables) — the article must make this clear early, or readers will conflate them
- Do not confuse Me dwarfs with Be stars: Be stars are hot B-type emission-line stars; Me stars are cool M-type — same "e" notation, completely different physics
- "Data are" — not "data is" throughout
- The "e" suffix in Mira variables (M IIIe) means emission from shocks, not from magnetic activity — the writer must not conflate the two mechanisms
- dMe stars are also UV Ceti-type and/or BY Draconis variables — but these are separate classification axes; dMe is a spectral label, not a variability class

3. dMe fraction statistics (5% → 100% across M subtypes)
4. Mechanism in dMe: rotation → dynamo → chromospheric/coronal heating → Balmer emission + flares
5. Mechanism in Me giants: pulsation → shock waves → emission near maximum light
6. Notable examples: Proxima Centauri, UV Ceti, EV Lacertae, Mira
7. Significance: dMe stars are exoplanet hosts; their flares have implications for habitability
