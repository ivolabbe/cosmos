# Jupiter — Article Spec

## Category
solar-system

## Definition
Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest in the Solar System — a gas giant whose mass (1.898 × 10²⁷ kg) exceeds that of all other planets combined by a factor of 2.5, with a rapid 9.9-hour rotation and the most powerful magnetic field of any planet. It is dominated by hydrogen and helium and hosts 95 officially recognised moons, including the four large Galilean satellites discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610.

## Full Content

### Physical Properties
- Mass: 1.898 × 10²⁷ kg — 317.8 times Earth's mass; more than twice all other planets combined [Wikipedia]
- Equatorial diameter: 142,976 km (11.2 times Earth's); polar radius 66,842 km vs equatorial 71,488 km — noticeably oblate due to rapid rotation [Wikipedia]
- Volume: 1.43 × 10¹⁵ km³ — ~1,321 Earths fit inside Jupiter [Wikipedia]
- Mean density: 1.326 g/cm³ — comparable to water, far lower than any terrestrial planet [Wikipedia]
- Surface gravity: 24.79 m/s² (2.53 times Earth's); escape velocity ~59.5 km/s [Wikipedia]
- Axial tilt: only 3.13°, so Jupiter experiences virtually no seasons [Wikipedia]
- Orbital period: 11.86 years; orbital eccentricity 0.049 (nearly circular) [Wikipedia]
- Mean distance from Sun: 778.5 million km (5.20 AU) [Wikipedia]
- Jupiter's barycenter with the Sun lies ~7% above the solar surface — the only planet whose centre of mass with the Sun sits outside the Sun itself [Wikipedia]
- Rotation period: ~9 hours 55 minutes (System III, tied to magnetic field); differential rotation — equatorial regions rotate ~5 minutes faster than polar regions [Wikipedia]
- Third-brightest natural object in the night sky after the Moon and Venus; mean apparent magnitude −2.20 [Wikipedia]

### Atmosphere
- Outer atmosphere (by volume): ~90% molecular hydrogen, ~10% helium; trace methane (0.3%), ammonia (0.026%), water vapour, hydrogen sulfide, ethane [Wikipedia]
- Bulk composition (interior estimate): 71% hydrogen, 24% helium, 5% heavier elements [Wikipedia]
- The atmosphere extends ~3,000 km below visible cloud tops, gradually transitioning from gas to liquid [Wikipedia]
- Cloud structure: the uppermost layer is ammonia ice crystals; below that, ammonium hydrosulfide clouds; water-ice and water-droplet clouds deeper still [Wikipedia/Britannica]
- Alternating light zones (anticyclonic, rising) and dark belts (cyclonic, descending) are driven by internal heat and rapid rotation [Wikipedia]
- Zonal jet streams reach wind speeds up to 100 m/s (360 km/h) [Wikipedia]
- Jupiter emits ~1.7 times more energy than it receives from the Sun, via slow gravitational contraction (Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism) — radiating internal heat accumulated during formation [Wikipedia]
- Cloud-top temperature at 1 bar: ~165 K (−108 °C) [Wikipedia]

### Great Red Spot and Storms
- The Great Red Spot (GRS) is an anticyclonic storm larger than Earth, rotating counterclockwise over ~6 days [Wikipedia]
- First reliably documented in 1831; possibly observed as early as 1665 by Cassini and Hooke [Wikipedia]
- Historical size ~41,000 km across (late 19th century); as of 2015 reduced to ~16,500 × 10,940 km; currently shrinking at ~930 km/year [Wikipedia]
- Juno measurements (2021) found the GRS extends 300–500 km deep below the cloud tops [Wikipedia]
- The GRS rises ~8 km above the surrounding cloud deck [Wikipedia]
- Oval BA ("Little Red Spot"): formed in 2000 from the merger of three white ovals; turned red by 2006; roughly half the GRS diameter [Wikipedia]
- Great Cold Spot: ~24,000 km across, ~200 °C cooler than surroundings; discovered at Jupiter's north pole in 2017; likely driven by auroral energy deposition [Wikipedia]
- Polar cyclones: nine cyclones arranged at the north pole (one central, eight surrounding); seven at the south pole — a stable geometric pattern confirmed by Juno [Wikipedia/NASA Science]

### Internal Structure
- No solid surface; pressure and temperature increase continuously with depth [Wikipedia]
- Outer molecular hydrogen layer gives way to metallic hydrogen at ~50–400 GPa and 5,000–8,400 K, beginning at roughly 80% of the planetary radius [Wikipedia]
- In the metallic hydrogen layer, electrons are freed from protons; the fluid conducts electricity like a metal and drives Jupiter's magnetic dynamo [Wikipedia]
- Diffuse core: 30–50% of the radius; contains heavy elements totalling 7–25 Earth masses [Wikipedia]
- Core temperature estimated ~20,000 K at ~4,000 GPa pressure [Wikipedia]
- Jupiter contracts by roughly 1 mm per year as part of ongoing Kelvin–Helmholtz cooling [Wikipedia]

### Magnetic Field
- Strongest planetary magnetic field in the Solar System; surface field 2–20 gauss (vs Earth's ~0.5 gauss) [Wikipedia]
- Dipole moment ~4.17 gauss·RJ³ (about 20,000 times Earth's) [Wikipedia]
- Magnetic axis tilted 10.31° from the rotation axis [Wikipedia]
- Generated by convective currents in the metallic hydrogen interior [Wikipedia]
- Magnetosphere extends ~75 Jupiter radii (RJ) to the sunward side; if visible from Earth it would subtend an angle larger than the full Moon [Britannica]
- All four Galilean moons orbit within the magnetosphere [Wikipedia]
- Io's volcanic outgassing (mainly SO₂) feeds a plasma torus of ionised sulfur and oxygen along its orbit [Wikipedia]
- Radio bursts from Jupiter's magnetosphere (0.6–30 MHz) can briefly exceed solar radio output when Earth lies in the emission cone [Wikipedia]
- Juno (2024–2026) has detected auroral footprints from all four Galilean moons, including Callisto's previously unconfirmed auroral signature [NASA Science]

### Ring System
- Jupiter has a faint ring system, first hinted at by Pioneer 10 (1974) and confirmed by Voyager 1 (1979) [Britannica]
- Three main components: an innermost diffuse halo, a narrow bright main ring (~6,400 km wide, ~30 km thick), and outer gossamer rings [Wikipedia]
- Rings are composed primarily of dust particles (not water ice like Saturn's rings) [Wikipedia]
- Main ring sourced from impact ejecta from small moons Metis and Adrastea [Wikipedia]
- Gossamer rings sourced from Thebe and Amalthea [Wikipedia]
- Rings are optically thin and dark; total mass negligible compared to Saturn's ring system [Wikipedia]

### Galilean Moons
- Galileo Galilei discovered Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto in January 1610 — the first satellites observed orbiting a body other than Earth [Wikipedia]
- **Io** (diameter 3,643 km): most volcanically active body in the Solar System; surface constantly resurfaced by sulfurous lava; orbital period 1.77 days [Wikipedia]
- **Europa** (3,122 km): geologically young, smooth icy surface with a global subsurface liquid-water ocean beneath; considered a prime target in the search for extraterrestrial life; orbital period 3.55 days [Wikipedia/NASA Science]
- **Ganymede** (5,262 km): largest moon in the Solar System, exceeding Mercury in diameter; has its own intrinsic magnetic field; orbital period 7.15 days [Wikipedia]
- **Callisto** (4,821 km): heavily cratered, ancient surface; also likely harbours a subsurface ocean; orbital period 16.69 days [Wikipedia]
- Laplace resonance: Io, Europa, and Ganymede maintain an orbital period ratio of 1:2:4, locked by their mutual gravitational interactions; tidal heating from this resonance powers Io's volcanism and Europa's internal ocean [Wikipedia]
- As of 2026 Jupiter has ~95 officially recognised moons (IAU) including many small irregular outer satellites [NASA Science]; some sources list 101 [Wikipedia]

### Exploration History
- Pioneer 10 (1973): first spacecraft to fly past Jupiter; measured the radiation environment; hinted at ring system [Britannica]
- Pioneer 11 (1974): confirmed Jupiter's magnetic field structure [Britannica]
- Voyager 1 and 2 (1979): detailed images of cloud structure, Great Red Spot, ring discovery, volcanic activity on Io, and surface detail of all four Galilean moons [Britannica]
- Galileo (1995–2003): first dedicated Jupiter orbiter; deployed an atmospheric probe that survived to ~130 km depth, measuring composition, temperature, and lightning; returned data on all four Galilean moons [Wikipedia]
- Cassini (2000): flyby en route to Saturn; provided high-resolution imaging [Wikipedia]
- New Horizons (2007): flyby en route to Pluto; imaged the Galilean moons with LORRI [Wikipedia/NASA]
- Juno (arrived July 2016, ongoing): polar orbit mission mapping Jupiter's gravitational and magnetic fields, atmospheric dynamics, auroras, and interior structure; extended mission running through at least 2025; conducts close flybys of Io, Europa, and Ganymede [NASA Science]
- Europa Clipper: launched October 2024, en route; will conduct ~50 flybys of Europa to assess habitability [NASA Science]
- JUICE (ESA): launched 2023; will orbit Ganymede by the mid-2030s [Wikipedia]

### Role in Solar System Dynamics
- Jupiter's gravitational dominance helped sculpt the early Solar System — its formation ~1 million years after the Sun is thought to have truncated the supply of material to the inner Solar System [Wikipedia]
- "Grand tack hypothesis": Jupiter migrated inward from ~3.5 AU to ~1.5 AU, then reversed outward when captured into 3:2 mean-motion resonance with Saturn; this migration scattered primordial planetesimals and may explain the small mass of Mars [Wikipedia]
- Jupiter's gravity shepherds the main asteroid belt, preventing planetesimals there from accreting into a planet; Kirkwood gaps in the belt correspond to orbital resonances with Jupiter [Wikipedia]
- Trojan asteroids: large swarms of asteroids share Jupiter's orbit at the L4 and L5 Lagrange points (~7,000 known Trojans) [Wikipedia; see also trojan-asteroids.html]
- Jupiter deflects many long-period comets from the inner Solar System — but can also redirect comets toward Earth (e.g., Comet Shoemaker–Levy 9 impacted Jupiter in July 1994 in a series of 21 strikes) [Wikipedia]
- Jupiter-family comets: short-period comets with orbital periods < 20 years, whose aphelia are near Jupiter's orbit and whose dynamics are controlled by Jupiter [see jupiter-family-comets.html]

## Historical Context
- Ancient astronomers tracked Jupiter as a bright wandering star; named for the Roman king of gods (Greek: Zeus)
- Galileo's 1610 discovery of the four large moons was the first evidence that heavenly bodies could orbit something other than Earth, dealing a blow to the geocentric model [Wikipedia]
- The Great Red Spot may have been observed by Giovanni Cassini in 1665, making it potentially a 350+ year old storm [Wikipedia]
- Comet Shoemaker–Levy 9 impact (July 1994): first directly observed collision between Solar System bodies; Jupiter's gravity had tidally disrupted the comet in 1992 into 21 fragments, each striking with enormous energy [Wikipedia]
