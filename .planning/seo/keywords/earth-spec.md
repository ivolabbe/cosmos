## Answer-first paragraph drafts

For each H2, a draft opening paragraph the writer can adapt to COSMOS voice.

### How big is the Earth? (~75K volume)
> Earth has an equatorial diameter of 12,742 km (7,918 miles) and a polar diameter of 12,714 km, making it the largest of the four terrestrial planets. Its total surface area is approximately 510.1 million km^2, of which about 71% is covered by oceans and 29% by land.

### What is the circumference of the Earth? (~55K volume)
> The circumference of the Earth at the equator is 40,075 km (24,901 miles). The polar circumference, measured around the poles, is slightly shorter at 40,008 km because Earth is not a perfect sphere but an oblate spheroid, bulging at the equator due to its rotation.

### What is the radius of the Earth? (~40K volume)
> Earth has a mean radius of 6,371 km. The equatorial radius is 6,378 km, while the polar radius is 6,357 km -- a difference of 21 km caused by the planet's rotational flattening. The Greek mathematician Eratosthenes first estimated Earth's radius around 240 BCE.

### What is the mass of the Earth? (~35K volume)
> The mass of the Earth is 5.972 x 10^24 kg (approximately 5.97 trillion trillion kilograms). This makes Earth the most massive of the terrestrial planets and roughly 330,000 times less massive than the Sun. Earth's high mean density of 5.51 g/cm^3 -- the highest of any planet in the Solar System -- indicates a substantial iron core.

### How was the Earth formed? (~25K volume)
> Earth formed approximately 4.54 billion years ago by accretion from the solar nebula, the disk of gas and dust that surrounded the young Sun. Over tens of millions of years, planetesimals collided and merged, building up the proto-Earth. Heat from impacts and radioactive decay caused the interior to differentiate into a metallic core and silicate mantle. A giant impact with a Mars-sized body roughly 4.5 billion years ago is thought to have produced the debris that coalesced into the Moon.

### Who named Earth? (~20K volume)
> The name "Earth" derives from the Old English word "eorthe" and the Germanic "erde", meaning ground or soil. Earth is the only planet in the Solar System whose English name does not derive from Greco-Roman mythology. There is no single person credited with naming the planet; the word evolved through common usage in the English language over centuries.

### What is Earth's atmosphere made of? (~20K volume)
> Earth's atmosphere is composed primarily of nitrogen (78.08%) and oxygen (20.95%), with trace amounts of argon (0.93%), carbon dioxide (0.04%), and variable water vapour. The atmosphere is divided into layers -- the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, and exosphere -- each defined by how temperature changes with altitude.

### What is the surface area of the Earth? (~12K volume)
> The total surface area of the Earth is approximately 510.1 million km^2 (196.9 million square miles). Of this, about 361 million km^2 (71%) is covered by water and 149 million km^2 (29%) is land. The oceans contain a total water volume of roughly 1.335 x 10^9 km^3.

### How fast does the Earth rotate? (~12K volume)
> Earth completes one rotation on its axis every 23 hours 56 minutes and 4 seconds (one sidereal day). At the equator, this corresponds to a surface speed of approximately 1,670 km/h (1,037 mph). The rotation speed decreases with latitude, reaching zero at the poles. Earth rotates from west to east, which is why the Sun appears to rise in the east.

### What is the Earth made of? (~10K volume)
> Earth is composed of an iron-nickel core, a thick silicate mantle, and a thin rocky crust. By mass, the planet is approximately 32% iron, 30% oxygen, 15% silicon, and 14% magnesium. The solid inner core reaches temperatures of approximately 5,400 degrees C, while the liquid outer core drives Earth's global magnetic field through a self-sustaining dynamo mechanism.

### How much of the Earth is water? (~10K volume)
> Approximately 71% of Earth's surface is covered by water, with the remaining 29% consisting of continents and islands. The total volume of water on Earth is about 1.335 x 10^9 km^3, of which 96.5% resides in the oceans. Only about 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh, and most of that is locked in ice caps and glaciers.

### What is the shape of the Earth? (~4.4K volume)
> Earth is an oblate spheroid -- a sphere slightly flattened at the poles and bulging at the equator. The equatorial diameter exceeds the polar diameter by about 43 km, giving a flattening ratio of approximately 1/298. This shape results from the centrifugal effect of Earth's rotation.

### Why does the Earth have seasons? (~4.4K volume)
> Seasons occur because Earth's rotational axis is tilted at 23.44 degrees relative to the perpendicular of its orbital plane. As Earth orbits the Sun, the Northern and Southern Hemispheres alternate between receiving more and less direct solar radiation, producing summer and winter respectively. The tilt, not Earth's varying distance from the Sun, is the primary cause of seasonal temperature changes.

### What is the temperature of the Earth? (~1.9K volume)
> The mean surface temperature of the Earth is approximately 15 degrees C (288 K / 59 degrees F). Surface temperatures range from a recorded minimum of -89.2 degrees C in Antarctica to a maximum of 56.7 degrees C in Death Valley, California. The greenhouse effect raises the mean temperature by about 33 degrees C above the bare radiative equilibrium value of -18 degrees C.
